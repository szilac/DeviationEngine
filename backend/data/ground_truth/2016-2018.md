# Ground-Truth World-State Report: 2016 to 2018

## I. Executive Summary

The years 2016 to 2018 began in the aftermath of a commodity-price collapse and a Chinese growth scare. They ended with the International Monetary Fund recording a broad but fraying global upswing. For contemporaries the most significant political developments were a cluster of electoral and constitutional shocks in the large states:

- **United Kingdom:** the June 2016 vote to leave the European Union.
- **United States:** Donald Trump's election in November 2016.
- **Turkey:** the failed coup of July 2016 and the two-year state of emergency that followed.
- **Brazil:** the removal of President Dilma Rousseff in August 2016.
- **India:** the November 2016 demonetisation.
- **China:** the October 2017 Party Congress that wrote "Xi Jinping Thought" into the party constitution, followed in March 2018 by the abolition of presidential term limits.
- **Ethiopia:** the April 2018 accession of Abiy Ahmed and rapid peace with Eritrea.
- **Brazil again:** Jair Bolsonaro's October 2018 victory.

Economically, the period saw the recovery the IMF dated from mid-2016, world growth that the IMF's Maurice Obstfeld said in October 2018 had "plateaued at 3.7 percent," and then in 2018 the arrival of the largest tariff measures between major economies in decades. Washington imposed Section 301 tariffs on $34 billion, then $16 billion, then $200 billion of Chinese imports between July and September 2018, and China retaliated in kind. India, China and much of Southeast Asia and East Africa kept growing fast. Brazil came out of what its statistics agency called its worst recession on record. Venezuela's economy collapsed into hyperinflation.

Socially, the period was defined by displacement. UNHCR's count of forcibly displaced people rose to 70.8 million at the end of 2018, most of them displaced inside their own countries. Asia and Africa set the demographic weight, and UN projections expected India's population to pass China's in the mid-2020s.

In knowledge and technique, the defining developments were:

- machine-learning systems beating the best human players at Go;
- the announcement of gravitational waves;
- China's quantum-communication satellite and large telescope;
- the spread of mobile payments and cheap data in China, India and Africa;
- a November 2018 claim from Shenzhen of the first gene-edited babies, which alarmed scientists worldwide.

In climate, the World Meteorological Organization recorded 2015–2018 as the four warmest years on record. The IPCC's October 2018 special report found that limiting warming to 1.5°C would need "unprecedented transitions in all aspects of society."

**Overall characterization.** This was a multipolarizing, Asia-weighted world. Growth was strong, but so were displacement and doubt about institutions. The post-1945 multilateral order was not overturned in these years, but its leading sponsor was questioning it, and its main challengers and hedgers were consolidating their own. How the trade conflict, the Korean diplomacy, the European Union's internal disputes and the Middle Eastern wars would end was genuinely unknown at the close of 2018.

## II. Global Overview

### Distribution of power

The units of power in this period were sovereign states and their blocs, plus a small number of non-state actors that held territory. The most important of these was the self-proclaimed Islamic State, which in 2016 still held Mosul and Raqqa and had lost both by late 2017.

**First rank.** Two states stood in the first rank by combined economic, military and technological weight.

- **The United States** spent $649 billion on its military in 2018 by SIPRI's estimate. That was 36 per cent of the world total and 2.6 times the next spender.
- **The People's Republic of China** spent an estimated $250 billion in 2018. SIPRI said this spending had grown for 24 consecutive years.

**Second rank.** A band of states and blocs had distinct regional or functional reach:

- the European Union, whose weight was economic and regulatory;
- Russia, whose weight was military, nuclear and energy-based, and expeditionary in Syria;
- India, whose weight was demographic, economic and military;
- Japan, whose weight was economic and technological;
- Saudi Arabia, whose weight was fiscal and energy-based. SIPRI ranked it third in military spending in 2018 at an estimated $67.6 billion.

**Regional powers.** A third band had real but more local weight: Turkey, Iran, Israel, Brazil, Indonesia, Nigeria, Ethiopia, South Africa, South Korea, Egypt, Pakistan and Mexico.

**How contemporaries read the relationships.**

- **US–China.** The relationship moved from wary engagement in 2016 to open strategic competition in 2018. Washington's own documents in 2017–2018 described China and Russia as strategic competitors.
- **Russia, Iran and Turkey.** From January 2017 this uneasy coalition ran the Astana talks on Syria. That sidelined the UN's Geneva track and US influence over the settlement.
- **The Gulf.** Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates aligned against both Iran and Qatar, and in June 2017 imposed a blockade on Qatar.
- **Europe.** The European Union was absorbed by Brexit negotiations, migration disputes and, from June 2018, a new Italian government sceptical of eurozone rules.
- **Africa.** The African Union, chaired in 2018 by Rwanda's Paul Kagame, pursued continental integration.

### Economy and exchange

**Production.** The world's goods were made above all in East Asia. China was the dominant assembler and a growing producer of components and capital goods, integrated with supply chains in South Korea, Japan, Taiwan and Southeast Asia. Other major producers:

- Europe (led by Germany) and North America in high-value manufacturing and services;
- the Gulf states, Russia, the United States (whose shale output was growing), Brazil and Africa's exporters in energy and raw materials;
- India in services and a large informal economy.

**Growth.** The IMF's October 2018 World Economic Outlook projected world growth of 3.7 per cent for 2018–19, the same as 2017, but 0.2 percentage points below its April forecast. It gave three reasons for the cut: "the negative effects of the trade measures," tighter financial conditions, and higher oil import bills. The IMF gave the 2017 average oil price as $52.81 a barrel and assumed $69.38 for 2018. That recovery from the 2014–16 collapse helped Gulf, Russian and Nigerian finances and hurt importers such as India.

**Disruptions** that contemporaries treated as major:

- **US–China tariffs.** US Section 301 tariffs of 25 per cent on $34 billion of Chinese goods (6 July 2018) and on $16 billion (23 August 2018), then 10 per cent on $200 billion (24 September 2018), scheduled to rise to 25 per cent in January 2019. China matched with retaliation on $34 billion, $16 billion and then $60 billion of US goods.
- **Other trade moves.** The US left the Trans-Pacific Partnership in January 2017. Eleven remaining members signed the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement (CPTPP) in March 2018. The US, Mexico and Canada renegotiated NAFTA as the USMCA in late 2018.
- **Emerging-market currency stress in 2018.** It was sharpest in Argentina and Turkey. Argentina turned to an IMF stand-by arrangement.
- **Venezuela's collapse.** On 23 July 2018 Alejandro Werner, the IMF's Western Hemisphere Director, wrote: "We are projecting a surge in inflation to 1,000,000 percent by end-2018 to signal that the situation in Venezuela is similar to that in Germany in 1923 or Zimbabwe in the late 2000's," and he stressed the forecast's "much higher degree of uncertainty."

**African integration.** On 21 March 2018 in Kigali, 44 of the 55 African Union member states signed the agreement establishing the African Continental Free Trade Area. Nigeria, the continent's largest economy, did not sign at that summit. Accounts of how many states signed the free-movement protocol that day differ: 27 in one law-firm summary, 30 in UNCTAD's and tralac's.

**Data quality.** Aggregate figures for this period are official releases, not reconstructions, but their reliability varies.

- **China.** China's National Bureau of Statistics reported growth of 6.7 per cent in 2016 and 6.9 per cent in 2017. Chinese authorities themselves acknowledged falsification in some provinces. Liaoning admitted inflating data for 2011–2014 and then reported a 2.5 per cent contraction for 2016. Tianjin and Inner Mongolia admitted inflating 2016 figures. Some foreign economists argued national growth was overstated. The NBS and a St. Louis Fed analysis argued the national series corrects for provincial overstatement. The level of true growth is contested.
- **Brazil.** IBGE first reported contractions of 3.8 per cent (2015) and 3.6 per cent (2016), then revised them in November 2018 to 3.5 and 3.3 per cent.
- **India.** India's post-2015 GDP series was debated domestically.
- **Venezuela.** Venezuela largely stopped publishing indicators. Estimates came from the IMF, the opposition-led National Assembly and outside economists.

### Social and demographic conditions

**Population.** UN DESA's World Population Prospects 2017 counted nearly 7.6 billion people in mid-2017, growing 1.10 per cent a year, or about 83 million people. By region:

- Asia: 4.5 billion
- Africa: 1.3 billion
- Europe: 742 million
- Latin America and the Caribbean: 646 million
- Northern America: 361 million
- Oceania: 41 million

The same revision projected that India would pass China in population around 2024 and that Nigeria would pass the United States before 2050.

**Displacement.** Forced displacement was the defining social emergency. UNHCR's Global Trends report counted 70.8 million forcibly displaced people at the end of 2018: 25.9 million refugees (5.5 million of them Palestinians under UNRWA), 3.5 million asylum-seekers, and some 41 million internally displaced. The largest displaced populations were:

- Syrians: 13.0 million
- Colombians: 8.0 million, almost all internal
- Congolese: 5.4 million
- Afghans: 5.1 million
- South Sudanese: 4.2 million

**Migration beyond flight from war.**

- Venezuelans moved to Colombia, Peru, Ecuador and Brazil.
- Central Americans travelled toward the United States in caravans in late 2018.
- Africans and Asians continued to cross the Mediterranean, although arrivals in Europe fell after the 2015–16 peak.
- Labour migrants from South and Southeast Asia kept going to the Gulf.

**Social and religious movements.**

- The #MeToo campaign spread from the United States in late 2017.
- Religious nationalism became more prominent in the politics of India, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Turkey and the United States.
- Saudi Arabia lifted its ban on women driving in June 2018.
- India's Supreme Court decriminalized homosexuality in September 2018.
- Ireland voted to repeal its constitutional abortion ban in May 2018.

### Environment and natural events

The World Meteorological Organization reported that 2015–2018 were the four warmest years on record. The global mean temperature for 2014–2018 was about 1.04°C above the pre-industrial baseline, and 2018 was the fourth-warmest year. The 2015–16 El Niño brought drought to southern and eastern Africa and parts of South and Southeast Asia in 2016.

The period's major natural disasters included:

- Atlantic hurricanes Harvey, Irma and Maria in 2017, which devastated Puerto Rico and several Caribbean states;
- severe monsoon flooding in Kerala in August 2018;
- the earthquake and tsunami at Palu, Sulawesi, in September 2018.

In February 2017 the UN warned of famine risk in Yemen, South Sudan, Somalia and north-eastern Nigeria, and famine was declared in parts of South Sudan. African swine fever reached China in August 2018, threatening the world's largest pig herd.

The IPCC's Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C was approved in Incheon on 6 October 2018. It was written by 91 authors and review editors from 40 countries and cited more than 6,000 references. It found that the world had already warmed about 1°C and that holding warming to 1.5°C would need unprecedented transitions.

### Flashpoints of the period (as they stood at the end of 2018)

1. **The Korean Peninsula.** In 2017 North Korea conducted its sixth nuclear test and tested intercontinental-range missiles, and rhetoric between Washington and Pyongyang reached the threat of war. The situation then reversed into summitry:
   - the Panmunjom Declaration (27 April 2018);
   - the first-ever US–DPRK summit in Singapore (12 June 2018), whose joint statement committed the DPRK only "to work toward complete denuclearization of the Korean Peninsula";
   - the Pyongyang summit (September 2018), with a military confidence-building agreement.

   Whether the diplomacy would hold or collapse was open. Critics noted that the Singapore outcome resembled China's long-proposed "dual suspension."
2. **Syria and the Levant.** Several rivalries converged here:
   - Russian- and Iranian-backed government forces retook three of the four Astana "de-escalation zones" in 2018, including Eastern Ghouta in April;
   - Idlib, with millions of civilians, was the last opposition-held zone, held under a Russian–Turkish arrangement;
   - Turkey fought Kurdish-led forces;
   - Israel struck Iranian targets inside Syria;
   - US forces remained in the north-east.
3. **The Gulf, Yemen and Iran.** On 8 May 2018 the United States withdrew from the 2015 nuclear agreement with Iran (the JCPOA), even though the IAEA had verified Iranian compliance in all four of its 2017 reports. US sanctions returned in August and November 2018. At the same time:
   - the Saudi-led coalition's war with the Houthis continued, including the 2018 offensive on the port of Hodeidah and the December 2018 Stockholm talks;
   - the Qatar blockade persisted;
   - the killing of the Saudi journalist Jamal Khashoggi in Istanbul in October 2018 strained Saudi Arabia's Western ties.
4. **US–China economic and maritime rivalry.** Tariffs, export controls and investment screening escalated. So did disputes over the South China Sea, where China rejected the July 2016 arbitral ruling in the Philippines' case, and over Taiwan. In December 2018 Canada arrested Huawei's chief financial officer, Meng Wanzhou, at US request.
5. **The Russia–West frontier.** The Donbas war in Ukraine was unresolved. In November 2018 Russian forces seized Ukrainian naval vessels near the Kerch Strait. Other sources of tension were the March 2018 nerve-agent poisoning in Salisbury, mass diplomatic expulsions, and US findings of Russian interference in its 2016 election.

A sixth zone had high potential for sudden escalation: **India–Pakistan–China**. It included the September 2016 Uri attack and India's announced cross-border "surgical strikes", and the 2017 India–China standoff at Doklam.

### Emerging forces (as a contemporary could judge them)

- **Platform and mobile-payment economies.** Examples were China's Alipay and WeChat Pay ecosystems, India's Jio-driven data boom after 2016, and mobile money in East Africa. Hundreds of millions of people were moving straight into digital finance.
- **Deep learning.** DeepMind's AlphaGo beat Lee Sedol in March 2016 and Ke Jie in 2017. China's 2017 national AI plan and heavy US corporate investment followed. Contemporaries argued whether this was a general-purpose technology or a hyped niche.
- **Gene editing.** CRISPR techniques spread rapidly. The November 2018 claim by He Jiankui of gene-edited twins drew near-universal condemnation, including from Chinese scientific bodies.
- **Cheap renewables and electric vehicles.** China led in solar manufacturing and in new-energy vehicle sales.
- **Cryptocurrencies.** Bitcoin boomed in late 2017 and fell sharply in 2018, alongside a wave of token offerings. Contemporaries were split on whether these were a financial innovation or a bubble.
- **New connectivity and development-finance institutions.** China's Belt and Road Initiative (with a Beijing forum in May 2017) and the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank. Some partners welcomed them, while others, pointing to Sri Lanka's 2017 lease of Hambantota port, warned of debt.
- **African continental integration** through the AfCFTA.

### Systemic fragilities

- **Dollar debt and monetary tightening.** Emerging-market dollar debt was exposed to US Federal Reserve rate rises, as 2018's currency crises in Argentina and Turkey showed.
- **China's high corporate and local-government debt.** Beijing's 2017–18 "deleveraging" campaign tried to contain it.
- **Petro-state dependence on oil prices.** Venezuela, Nigeria, Angola and Iraq depended on oil prices. Venezuela, by one contemporary estimate, relied on crude for 96 per cent of its revenue.
- **Maritime chokepoints**, above all Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, adjacent to the Gulf confrontation and the Yemen war.
- **Strain on the trade system.** The US blocked appointments to the WTO Appellate Body.
- **Epidemic exposure in conflict zones.** Yemen's cholera epidemic was one example. Another was the Ebola outbreak declared in North Kivu, DRC, in August 2018 amid armed conflict. The response used the experimental rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine.

## III. Major Powers and Regional Blocs

### People's Republic of China

**Internal politics.**

- At the 19th Party Congress (18–24 October 2017), Xi Jinping was confirmed for a second term as general secretary. No designated successor emerged.
- The Congress wrote "Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era" into the party constitution. This was the first such honour for a living leader since Mao.
- The Congress redefined the "principal contradiction" of Chinese society as that between "the people's ever-growing need for a better life and unbalanced and inadequate development." Chinese theorists read the new formula as a shift from quantity to quality of growth.
- In March 2018 the National People's Congress amended the state constitution to remove the two-term limit on the presidency.
- The anti-corruption campaign continued and was institutionalized in a new National Supervisory Commission in 2018.
- In Xinjiang, authorities expanded a security system that the government described as vocational training and counter-extremism. A UN treaty body in August 2018 cited credible reports of mass arbitrary detention of Uyghurs. The scale was contested and difficult to verify.

**External relations.**

- The Belt and Road Initiative was Beijing's flagship.
- China built and militarized islands in the South China Sea after rejecting the 2016 arbitral award.
- It deepened its alignment with Russia.
- It signed a provisional agreement with the Holy See on bishop appointments in September 2018.
- In 2018 it faced US tariffs, to which it responded with calibrated retaliation and negotiations led by Vice Premier Liu He.

**Economic basis.**

- China was the world's largest manufacturer and goods trader.
- Growth was officially 6.7 per cent (2016) and 6.9 per cent (2017), and the IMF projected 6.6 per cent for 2018. The true level is contested, as noted above.
- Policy aimed at "supply-side structural reform," cuts to overcapacity in steel and coal, and pollution control.
- Official figures showed large falls in Beijing's fine-particulate pollution in winter 2017.

**Knowledge and technique.**

- The Micius quantum-communication satellite launched in 2016.
- The FAST radio telescope was completed in 2016.
- The Sunway TaihuLight supercomputer topped world rankings from 2016 until mid-2018.
- The Made in China 2025 industrial programme and the 2017 AI development plan set national technology goals.
- Huawei became a leading 5G equipment supplier.

**Key individuals beyond Xi.**

- Premier Li Keqiang.
- Wang Qishan, anti-corruption chief and, from 2018, vice president.
- Liu He, economic adviser and trade negotiator.
- Wang Huning, ideologist.
- People's Bank of China governors Zhou Xiaochuan and, from 2018, Yi Gang.
- Entrepreneurs Jack Ma (Alibaba), Pony Ma (Tencent) and Ren Zhengfei (Huawei).
- He Jiankui, whose gene-editing claim shaped global debate.

**Key organizations.**

- The Communist Party's Central Committee and Politburo Standing Committee.
- The Central Commission for Discipline Inspection.
- The People's Liberation Army, reorganized into theatre commands from 2016.
- State-owned banks and the national champion firms.

### United States

**Internal politics.**

- Donald Trump won the November 2016 election on the Electoral College while losing the popular vote.
- He governed through executive actions on immigration and trade, and signed the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act in December 2017.
- A special counsel investigation, begun in May 2017, examined Russian interference and possible links to the campaign.
- Democrats won the House of Representatives in the November 2018 midterms.
- The opioid overdose epidemic was the most prominent domestic health crisis.

**External relations.** The administration:

- withdrew from the TPP (2017);
- announced its intention to leave the Paris climate agreement (June 2017);
- recognized Jerusalem as Israel's capital (December 2017) and moved its embassy there (May 2018);
- left the JCPOA (May 2018);
- imposed steel and aluminium tariffs on allies and Section 301 tariffs on China (2018);
- held the first summit with North Korea's leader (June 2018);
- renegotiated NAFTA as the USMCA;
- blocked WTO appellate appointments.

**Economic basis.**

- The US had the world's largest economy by market exchange rates and was the issuer of the reserve currency.
- It was the largest military spender.
- Shale made it a leading oil and gas producer.
- The IMF projected 2.9 per cent growth for 2018, buoyed by fiscal stimulus.
- The Federal Reserve raised rates steadily, under Janet Yellen and, from February 2018, Jerome Powell.

**Knowledge and technique.** The US led in semiconductor design, cloud computing and AI research through firms including Google, whose DeepMind unit was based in London. It also led in biotechnology and space: SpaceX's Falcon Heavy flew in February 2018. The LIGO collaboration announced the first detection of gravitational waves in February 2016.

**Key individuals beyond Trump.** Secretaries of State Rex Tillerson and Mike Pompeo, Defense Secretary James Mattis, Trade Representative Robert Lighthizer, special counsel Robert Mueller, and Mark Zuckerberg, whose Facebook faced the March 2018 Cambridge Analytica data scandal.

### India

**Internal politics.**

- Prime Minister Narendra Modi's BJP government dominated national politics and expanded its reach, winning Uttar Pradesh in 2017.
- On 8 November 2016 it withdrew ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes, about 86 per cent of currency in circulation, to fight "black money," counterfeiting and terror financing. The Reserve Bank of India's 2017–18 annual report found that 99.3 per cent of the value (₹15.31 lakh crore of ₹15.42 lakh crore) came back into the banking system. Critics took that as evidence the goal of extinguishing illicit cash had failed. The government argued the measure widened the tax base and pushed payments toward digital channels.
- A nationwide Goods and Services Tax began in July 2017.
- The government also launched the Aadhaar-linked welfare payments and a national health-insurance scheme, Ayushman Bharat, in 2018.
- The Supreme Court decriminalized homosexuality and allowed women of all ages into the Sabarimala temple (September 2018).
- RBI Governor Urjit Patel resigned in December 2018 amid disputes with the government.

**External relations.**

- India faced a standoff with China at Doklam from 16 June to 28 August 2017, when India's Ministry of External Affairs announced that "expeditious disengagement of border personnel at the face-off site at Doklam has been agreed to and is ongoing."
- It launched cross-border "surgical strikes" against Pakistan after the September 2016 Uri attack.
- It deepened ties with the US and Japan.
- It stayed out of the Belt and Road Initiative.
- It kept buying Russian arms and ranked fourth in SIPRI's 2018 military-spending list.

**Economic basis.**

- India had a large services and IT export sector, a vast informal and agrarian economy, and pharmaceuticals.
- Growth was fast but disrupted by demonetisation and the GST rollout.
- The GDP series and informal-sector effects are debated. One Indian academic assessment called demonetisation "the single largest factor" affecting growth in 2016–18, but others attribute the slowdown to the GST and bank bad-debt problems.

**Knowledge and technique.**

- The Indian Space Research Organisation launched 104 satellites on one rocket in February 2017.
- The Unified Payments Interface and Reliance Jio's low-cost 4G transformed digital access.

**Key individuals.**

- Finance Minister Arun Jaitley and BJP president Amit Shah.
- RBI governors Raghuram Rajan (until September 2016) and Urjit Patel.
- Mukesh Ambani of Reliance.
- Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi.

### European Union and the major European states

**Internal politics.**

- On 23 June 2016 the United Kingdom voted to leave the EU by 51.9 to 48.1 per cent (17,410,742 to 16,141,241 votes, on 72.2 per cent turnout), according to the Electoral Commission's official declaration. It triggered Article 50 in March 2017, and in November 2018 Theresa May's government agreed a withdrawal text that her own parliament had not yet approved.
- Emmanuel Macron won France's presidency in May 2017. His reforms faced the "gilets jaunes" protests from November 2018.
- Germany's September 2017 election brought the Alternative für Deutschland into the Bundestag and produced a prolonged coalition negotiation.
- Italy's March 2018 election produced a Five Star–League government that clashed with Brussels over its budget.
- Governments in Poland and Hungary contested EU rule-of-law procedures.

**External relations.**

- The EU defended the JCPOA against US withdrawal and kept sanctions on Russia.
- It negotiated trade agreements with Japan and Canada.
- It sought migration deals with Turkey and North African states.

**Economic basis.** The EU was the world's largest single market and a regulatory power. Its General Data Protection Regulation took effect in May 2018. The ECB under Mario Draghi kept quantitative easing going until announcing its end for December 2018.

**Key individuals.** Angela Merkel, Emmanuel Macron, Theresa May, Commission President Jean-Claude Juncker, chief Brexit negotiator Michel Barnier, Mario Draghi, Viktor Orbán and Matteo Salvini.

**Key organizations.** The European Commission, the European Central Bank, NATO (whose 29 members spent $963 billion, 53 per cent of world military spending, in 2018 per SIPRI), and national parliaments.

### Russian Federation

**Internal politics.**

- Vladimir Putin was re-elected in March 2018. Opposition figure Alexei Navalny was barred from standing.
- A pension-age increase in 2018 provoked protests.
- Russia hosted the 2018 FIFA World Cup.

**External relations.**

- Russia's 2015 intervention in Syria gave it the lead diplomatic role through the Astana process, launched in January 2017 with Iran and Turkey, and the January 2018 Sochi congress.
- The Donbas conflict and the Crimea question remained unresolved.
- The Kerch Strait incident of November 2018 heightened tension.
- Western sanctions expanded after the 2018 Salisbury poisoning and US findings of election interference.
- Russia cooperated with Saudi Arabia in the OPEC-plus output agreements from late 2016.

**Economic basis.** Russia depended on oil, gas, metals, grain and arms exports. SIPRI recorded Russia making the largest decrease in military spending of any country in 2017.

**Key individuals.** Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov, Defence Minister Sergei Shoigu, Chief of the General Staff Valery Gerasimov, Central Bank Governor Elvira Nabiullina and Alexei Navalny.

### Japan and the Korean Peninsula

**Japan.**

- Prime Minister Shinzō Abe's government pursued "Abenomics."
- It led the CPTPP after US withdrawal.
- It signed an economic partnership agreement with the EU.
- It debated constitutional revision.
- Emperor Akihito's wish to abdicate, expressed in August 2016, led to a 2017 law setting the abdication for 2019.
- Japan's demographic ageing was the most advanced of any major state.

**South Korea.**

- President Park Geun-hye was impeached after mass candlelight protests and removed in March 2017.
- Moon Jae-in, elected in May 2017, led the inter-Korean diplomacy of 2018.

**North Korea.**

- Under Kim Jong Un, North Korea conducted its sixth nuclear test in September 2017.
- It launched the Hwasong-15 missile in November 2017.
- It then declared a halt to testing and joined the 2018 summitry.

### The Middle East: Saudi Arabia, Iran, Turkey, Israel, Egypt

**Saudi Arabia.**

- Mohammed bin Salman became crown prince in June 2017.
- He launched Vision 2030 diversification.
- In November 2017 he detained hundreds of princes and businessmen at the Riyadh Ritz-Carlton in what the government called an anti-corruption drive.
- The women's driving ban was lifted.
- He led the Yemen war and the Qatar blockade.
- The Khashoggi killing of October 2018 damaged the kingdom's international standing.
- SIPRI ranked Saudi Arabia's 2018 military burden at 8.8 per cent of GDP, the world's highest.

**Iran.**

- President Hassan Rouhani was re-elected in 2017 on a platform of engagement.
- Protests over economic conditions spread across provincial cities in December 2017 and January 2018.
- After the May 2018 US withdrawal from the JCPOA, returning sanctions and currency depreciation pushed Iran into recession. SIPRI recorded a 9.5 per cent fall in Iranian military spending in 2018.
- Key figures: Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, Foreign Minister Mohammad Javad Zarif, and Quds Force commander Qasem Soleimani, who directed Iran's regional networks in Iraq, Syria, Lebanon and Yemen.

**Turkey.**

- A faction of the armed forces attempted a coup on 15 July 2016. Turkish officials counted 251 people killed and 2,194 wounded resisting it; other tallies exceed 300.
- The government blamed the movement of the US-based cleric Fethullah Gülen.
- A state of emergency ran from July 2016 to July 2018, with 32 emergency decrees. More than 130,000 people were dismissed from the military, police, civil service and academia.
- An April 2017 referendum approved an executive presidency, which took effect after President Erdoğan's June 2018 re-election.
- Turkey's 2018 military spending rose 24 per cent (SIPRI).
- It ran cross-border operations in northern Syria in 2016–18.
- Its currency came under severe pressure in 2018.

**Israel.**

- Under Benjamin Netanyahu, Israel gained US recognition of Jerusalem.
- It passed the Nation-State Law (July 2018).
- It struck Iranian targets in Syria.
- It faced the Gaza "Great March of Return" protests from March 2018, in which many Palestinians were killed by Israeli fire.

**Egypt.**

- Under Abdel Fattah el-Sisi, Egypt floated its pound and took a large IMF loan in November 2016.
- Sisi was re-elected in 2018.
- The state fought an insurgency in North Sinai.

**Iraq.**

- Iraqi forces retook Mosul from the Islamic State in July 2017.
- The Kurdistan Region's September 2017 independence referendum was followed by federal forces retaking Kirkuk.

### Southeast Asia

**Philippines.**

- President Rodrigo Duterte's "war on drugs" was the region's most contested internal policy. Estimates of the dead differ by a factor of five or more:
  - the Philippine Drug Enforcement Agency counted 4,948 suspects killed in police operations from July 2016 to 30 September 2018, rising to 5,104 by the end of 2018;
  - Human Rights Watch estimated more than 12,000 dead, including victims of "unidentified gunmen," by January 2018;
  - the head of the Commission on Human Rights said in December 2018 that the toll could be as high as 27,000. Government officials rejected that figure as unsupported.
- The 2017 siege of Marawi by Islamic State–aligned fighters lasted five months.
- Duterte improved relations with China despite the Philippines' 2016 arbitral victory.

**Myanmar.**

- Under State Counsellor Aung San Suu Kyi and the autonomous military (Tatmadaw) led by Min Aung Hlaing, military "clearance operations" in northern Rakhine followed insurgent attacks on 25 August 2017.
- UNHCR reported that "some 688,000 refugees have fled Myanmar to Bangladesh since 25 August 2017" as of 20 February 2018. The 2018 Joint Response Plan counted 671,000 by early 2018. They joined some 200,000 earlier refugees in camps at Cox's Bazar, which became the largest refugee settlement in the world.
- A UN fact-finding mission in 2018 called for senior generals to be investigated for genocide. Myanmar's government rejected its findings.

**Malaysia.** The May 2018 election ended the Barisan Nasional's six-decade rule amid the 1MDB scandal and returned Mahathir Mohamad as prime minister.

**Indonesia.**

- Under Joko Widodo, the world's most populous Muslim-majority democracy saw Jakarta's governor Basuki Tjahaja Purnama jailed for blasphemy in 2017 after mass Islamist mobilization.
- It suffered the Palu disaster in 2018.

**Thailand.** King Bhumibol Adulyadej died in October 2016 after 70 years on the throne. The military government remained in power.

**Vietnam and ASEAN.** Vietnam kept up rapid export-led growth. ASEAN negotiated with China over a South China Sea code of conduct.

### Sub-Saharan Africa and the African Union

**Ethiopia.**

- After years of protest, especially in Oromia and Amhara, Prime Minister Hailemariam Desalegn resigned in early 2018.
- Abiy Ahmed became EPRDF chairman on 27 March 2018 and prime minister on 2 April.
- He released thousands of political prisoners, delisted armed opposition groups such as Ginbot 7, the OLF and the ONLF, and announced partial liberalization of state firms.
- He made peace with Eritrea at a July 2018 summit, formalized in a Jeddah agreement in September.
- Ethnic displacement inside Ethiopia rose sharply in 2018, which UNHCR noted.

**Nigeria.**

- Nigeria was Africa's most populous state and a major oil producer.
- Under Muhammadu Buhari it came out of a 2016 recession.
- It fought Boko Haram and the Islamic State's West Africa affiliate in the north-east.
- It faced farmer–herder violence in the Middle Belt.
- It hesitated over the AfCFTA.
- Aliko Dangote's industrial group was building a large refinery.

**South Africa.**

- Jacob Zuma resigned in February 2018 under pressure over "state capture." Cyril Ramaphosa succeeded him.
- South Africa signed the AfCFTA in mid-2018.

**Other transitions and conflicts.**

- Zimbabwe's military forced Robert Mugabe out in November 2017.
- Angola's long-serving president José Eduardo dos Santos stepped down in 2017.
- The Gambia's Yahya Jammeh left after losing an election and a regional intervention.
- The DRC's delayed election was held on 30 December 2018 amid conflict and Ebola.
- South Sudan signed a revitalized peace agreement in September 2018.
- Cameroon's Anglophone regions slid into armed conflict.
- Sahelian states formed the G5 Sahel joint force against jihadist groups.
- Protests over bread prices began in Sudan in December 2018.

**African Union.** Under Commission Chair Moussa Faki Mahamat and 2018 chair Paul Kagame, the AU launched the AfCFTA, with Niger's Mahamadou Issoufou as its champion. The UN Economic Commission for Africa projected large gains in intra-African trade.

**External partners.** China's September 2018 Forum on China–Africa Cooperation pledged new finance.

### Latin America

**Brazil.**

- IBGE described the 2015–16 recession as the worst in its records.
- Amid the Lava Jato corruption investigations, the Senate removed President Dilma Rousseff on 31 August 2016 by 61 votes to 20, on charges of fiscal-rule violations. Her supporters called the process a parliamentary coup.
- Michel Temer governed with an austerity agenda.
- Former president Lula was jailed in April 2018 and barred from running.
- Jair Bolsonaro won the October 2018 run-off with 55.13 per cent of valid votes against Fernando Haddad.
- Judge Sérgio Moro, who had led Lava Jato trials, was named his justice minister.

**Mexico.**

- Mexico faced record homicide levels.
- It renegotiated NAFTA.
- In July 2018 it elected Andrés Manuel López Obrador.

**Venezuela.**

- Under Nicolás Maduro, Venezuela convened a Constituent Assembly in 2017 and held a May 2018 presidential election that many neighbours rejected.
- Oil output kept falling.
- The IMF projected an 18 per cent GDP contraction in 2018.
- Inflation estimates varied widely by method. The IMF's Alejandro Werner projected 1,000,000 per cent by end-2018. Economist Steve Hanke, using a purchasing-power-parity measure, wrote on 1 January 2019 that "Venezuela's annual inflation rate at the end of 2018 was 80,000%." The National Assembly's estimates were higher.
- UNHCR and IOM counted 3 million Venezuelans abroad by November 2018.
- The government attributed the crisis to an "economic war" by domestic and foreign opponents.

**Colombia.** Colombia ratified a revised peace agreement with the FARC in November 2016, after voters narrowly rejected the first version. It elected Iván Duque in 2018.

**Argentina.** Under Mauricio Macri, Argentina turned to a $50 billion IMF stand-by arrangement in June 2018 amid currency stress. It was later enlarged.

## IV. Thematic Sections

### Knowledge, science, and technology

**Physics and astronomy.**

- The LIGO–Virgo collaborations detected gravitational waves, announced in February 2016, and in 2017 observed a neutron-star merger across the electromagnetic spectrum.
- China's FAST telescope began operating.
- China's Micius satellite demonstrated quantum key distribution between continents.

**Artificial intelligence.** AlphaGo's 2016–17 victories made machine learning a subject of state strategy in China, the US and Europe. Contemporaries were split between those who saw a general-purpose technology and those who saw hype.

**Biomedicine.**

- CRISPR techniques spread widely. The He Jiankui affair in November 2018 prompted calls for a moratorium on heritable editing.
- The 2018 Nobel Prize in medicine went to James Allison and Tasuku Honjo for cancer immunotherapy.

**Digital infrastructure and space.**

- Mobile payments in China, India's UPI and Aadhaar systems, and African mobile money made the Global South a site of financial-technology innovation, not just adoption.
- The first 5G standard release was completed in 2018.
- India's ISRO set a record with 104 satellites on one launch.
- SpaceX flew reusable boosters and the Falcon Heavy.
- China launched the Chang'e-4 lunar far-side mission in December 2018.

### Health and population

**Outbreaks.**

- The Zika epidemic was a WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern from February to November 2016.
- Yemen's cholera epidemic passed one million suspected cases by late 2017. WHO reported that 99.7 per cent of patients who reached care survived. Diphtheria followed.
- Ebola appeared in DRC's Équateur province in May 2018 and in North Kivu from August 2018, in an active conflict zone.
- Measles returned in Venezuela and parts of Europe.

**Chronic crises.**

- Opioid overdoses in the United States.
- Malnutrition in Yemen, South Sudan, north-east Nigeria and Somalia.
- Health collapse in Venezuela.
- Crowding and disease risk in the Rohingya camps.

**Demography.** Global life expectancy had risen to 69 years for men and 73 for women (2010–2015, UN DESA). The population of the least developed countries was projected to keep growing fast, while China, Japan, Korea and Europe were ageing.

### Security and conflict

**Wars and death tolls.** The deadliest wars were in Syria, Yemen, Afghanistan, Iraq (against the Islamic State), South Sudan, the DRC, Nigeria's north-east and the Sahel. Casualty counts are contested and should be read as ranges:

- **Syria.** The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights estimated up to 511,000 deaths by March 2018, including the missing. Other contemporary tallies ranged from about 350,000 to over 400,000 documented deaths. UNHCR counted 6.6 million internally displaced and 5.6 million refugees.
- **Yemen.** A figure of "more than 10,000" deaths circulated widely in 2017 (Khaleej Times cited 8,750). Later ACLED-based studies counted many more. Contemporaries agreed the count was a serious undercount because of limited access.

**Diplomacy.**

- The Astana process eclipsed the Geneva track on Syria. The UN envoy changed at the end of 2018.
- The Korean summits of 2018 opened a diplomatic track.
- The Ethiopia–Eritrea peace ended a two-decade standoff.
- South Sudan's revitalized agreement and Colombia's FARC accord showed negotiated settlements were still possible.

**Military technique and organization.**

- Armed drones spread to many state and non-state actors, including the Houthis and the Islamic State.
- Russia and the US both modernized their nuclear forces.
- China reorganized the PLA.
- Precision strikes and cyber operations became routine instruments.
- Global military spending, according to SIPRI, rose in 2018 to its highest level since 1988.
- Six of the ten countries with the highest military burden were in the Middle East.

### Culture and society

**Film and popular culture.** Culture industries outside the West had reach well beyond their home markets: China's box office hits such as *Wolf Warrior 2* (2017); Indian films' success in China; Nollywood; and the Korean pop wave, in which BTS became global stars.

**Religion.**

- Pope Francis's Vatican reached a deal with Beijing.
- Hindu, Buddhist and Islamic revivalist politics shaped elections in South and Southeast Asia.
- Pakistan's Supreme Court acquitted Asia Bibi of blasphemy in October 2018, prompting mass protests by Tehreek-e-Labbaik.

**Social movements and information.**

- #MeToo, Saudi women's driving, India's decriminalization of homosexuality and Ireland's abortion referendum marked shifts in gender and sexuality norms, alongside strong conservative counter-movements in Russia, Brazil, Poland and elsewhere.
- Contemporaries debated "post-truth" politics, "fake news" and the power of social-media platforms, from the 2016 US election to lynchings linked to WhatsApp rumours in India in 2018.

## V. Conclusion

At the end of 2018 an informed observer would have described a world that was growing but anxious. Asia held most of the world's people and was the core of its manufacturing. The United States was still unmatched militarily and financially but was using tariffs and sanctions more than coalitions. China had consolidated power at home and projected it through connectivity finance and technology. Europe was preoccupied with internal division. Russia, Turkey, Iran and Saudi Arabia were assertive regional actors in overlapping theatres.

Africa's integration project and Ethiopia's opening were read as signs of a continent choosing its own direction. Latin America had swung from the left toward the right in Brazil and the left in Mexico, while absorbing Venezuela's exodus.

The open questions were real and unresolved:

- Would the US–China tariff escalation settle into a deal or widen into a technology and financial contest?
- Would the Korean diplomacy produce verifiable arms control or collapse back into testing?
- How would Britain leave the EU, and would it leave at all?
- Would Idlib and Hodeidah see negotiated pauses or major offensives?
- Would Iran stay in the JCPOA under renewed sanctions?
- Would the Federal Reserve's tightening trigger wider emerging-market crises?
- Could the 1.5°C pathway described by the IPCC be reconciled with the fossil-fuel expansion then under way?

None of these outcomes was known, and each had plausible alternatives.
