# Ground-Truth World-State Report: 2018 to 2020

## I. Executive Summary

Seen from the end of 2020, the period had two parts. In 2018–2019 the world economy was still growing, but a trade confrontation between the two largest economies was steadily eroding the rules. In 2020 a pandemic forced almost every society into emergency government at the same time.

**Politics.** The central fact of the period was the open rivalry between the United States and the People's Republic of China, fought mainly through trade and technology rather than force. From 2018 Washington imposed tariffs on successive lists of Chinese goods, and Beijing retaliated. The "Phase One" agreement, signed on 15 January 2020 by President Donald Trump and Vice Premier Liu He, eased the war without ending it. US tariffs of 25 percent on about $250 billion of Chinese goods stayed in place, and China committed to buy an extra $200 billion of US goods and services over 2020–2021 above 2017 levels. In parallel, China imposed a National Security Law on Hong Kong on 30 June 2020 after the 2019 protest movement; India reorganized Jammu and Kashmir in 2019 and fought a deadly clash with China in the Galwan Valley in June 2020; Russia amended its constitution in 2020; the United Kingdom left the European Union at the end of January 2020; long-standing rulers fell in Algeria and Sudan in 2019; and war broke out in Ethiopia's Tigray region in November 2020.

**Economy.** Before COVID-19 the main economic worries were trade friction, slowing Chinese growth and high debt. In 2020 the world entered what the IMF called "the Great Lockdown." The IMF's October 2020 outlook projected global growth of −4.4 percent for 2020: −5.8 percent for advanced economies and −5.7 percent for emerging and developing economies excluding China. China officially grew 2.3 percent. India reported a 23.9 percent year-on-year fall in output in April–June 2020. ECLAC estimated a 7.7 percent contraction for Latin America and the Caribbean, the largest in 120 years, and the World Bank projected Sub-Saharan Africa's first recession in 25 years. The World Bank estimated that the pandemic would push an extra 88 to 115 million people into extreme poverty in 2020. Oil prices briefly went negative in the United States in April 2020.

**Society.** Forced displacement reached a record 79.5 million people at the end of 2019, according to UNHCR. Mass protest movements rose in many places at once, especially in 2019: Hong Kong, Chile, Lebanon, Iraq, Algeria, Sudan and elsewhere. Lockdowns in 2020 broke up many of these movements and created new grievances, including #EndSARS in Nigeria, anti-racism protests that spread outward from the United States, and farmers' protests in India at the end of the year.

**Knowledge and technique.** Scientific and technological competition was increasingly organized around the US–China relationship, especially in semiconductors, 5G, artificial intelligence and space. The biomedical response to COVID-19 was the fastest vaccine development ever recorded: by December 2020 vaccines built on several platforms (mRNA, adenoviral vector and inactivated virus) had been authorized by regulators in the United Kingdom, the United States, Russia, China and elsewhere.

**Environment.** WMO reported that 2020 was one of the three warmest years on record, at about 14.9 °C, or 1.2 (± 0.1) °C above the 1850–1900 level, and that 2011–2020 was the warmest decade on record. The period also brought major bushfires in Australia, fires in the Amazon, the worst desert locust upsurge in decades across the Horn of Africa, Arabia and Southwest Asia, and Cyclone Idai in southeastern Africa.

**Overall characterization.** At the end of 2020 the world was highly interconnected but becoming more defensive. It was multipolar in its economy but dominated by two powers in strategy, and it had been hit simultaneously by a public-health emergency that states were handling with very different capacities and results. Asia held most of the world's people and manufacturing. The institutions of the post-1945 and post-1990 order, including the WTO, WHO and arms-control treaties, were under open strain.

## II. Global Overview

### Distribution of power

**Two leading powers.** SIPRI estimated world military spending at $1,917 billion in 2019, the highest level since 1988 and 3.6 percent higher in real terms than 2018. The United States spent $732 billion (38 percent of the world total). China spent an estimated $261 billion, the second-largest total; this is a SIPRI estimate, not an official Chinese number. Together the two accounted for more than half of global military spending. The United States led in military reach, alliances and control of the dollar-based financial system. China was the world's largest manufacturer and the largest trading partner of many countries, and on IMF purchasing-power-parity measures its economy was already the largest.

**Other major powers.**
- **Russia** was the fourth-largest military spender in 2019 at $65.1 billion, with a military burden of 3.9 percent of GDP (SIPRI). It had nuclear parity with the United States, a military presence in Syria, a growing role in Libya and parts of Africa, and in November 2020 it brokered the South Caucasus ceasefire.
- **India** was the third-largest military spender in 2019 at $71.1 billion, and its population of well over a billion was close to China's.
- **The European Union** was a single trading and regulatory power but not a unified military actor. France spent $50.1 billion on its military in 2019, and Germany's spending rose 10 percent to $49.3 billion, the largest increase among the top fifteen spenders.
- **Japan** ($47.6 billion) and **South Korea** ($43.9 billion) were the largest Asian military spenders after China and India.
- **Middle powers** included Saudi Arabia (an estimated $61.9 billion in 2019, by far the largest spender in its region), Turkey, Iran, the UAE, Israel, Brazil, Indonesia, Nigeria, South Africa and Ethiopia.

**Blocs and institutions.** The main groupings were NATO (29 members in 2019, with combined spending of $1,035 billion), the EU, ASEAN, the African Union, the Gulf Cooperation Council (split by the Saudi-led embargo of Qatar from 2017), the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, and the loosely coordinated "Quad" of the United States, Japan, India and Australia. China's Belt and Road Initiative gave Beijing a network of financing and construction relationships across Eurasia, Africa and beyond; some borrowing states began renegotiating debts in 2020.

**Non-state and quasi-state actors.** Many exercised real territorial or economic power:
- the Taliban, which negotiated directly with the United States;
- the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah), which controlled Sanaa and much of northern Yemen;
- Hezbollah;
- the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces;
- jihadist groups across the Sahel, the Lake Chad basin, Somalia (al-Shabaab), northern Mozambique and the southern Philippines (the Islamic State group lost its last Syrian territory in March 2019 but continued an insurgency);
- rival authorities in Libya;
- criminal armed groups in parts of Mexico, Central America and Brazil.

In Libya, Yemen, Somalia, Syria, Afghanistan, parts of the Sahel and the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, the national government did not control large parts of its formal territory.

**Platform companies.** A small number of US and Chinese technology firms (Alphabet/Google, Apple, Amazon, Facebook, Microsoft; Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, Huawei) ran communications, payments, cloud and information infrastructure and were increasingly treated as instruments or targets of state strategy: Washington added Huawei to its Entity List in May 2019 and moved against TikTok and WeChat in 2020, while Beijing was tightening regulation of its own platform firms by end-2020.

### Economy and exchange

**Pre-pandemic structure (2018–2019).** Production was organized through global value chains centered on East Asia (China, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and increasingly Vietnam), North America and the EU. Commodity exporters in the Gulf, Russia, Latin America, Africa and Australia supplied energy, metals and food, with China the marginal buyer for many of these goods. The main disruptions came from trade policy:
- the US–China tariff escalation;
- renegotiation of NAFTA into the USMCA, which took effect on 1 July 2020;
- paralysis of the WTO's Appellate Body from December 2019, after the United States blocked new appointments;
- Brexit.

China's National Bureau of Statistics revised its 2019 growth figure down from 6.1 to 6.0 percent, mainly in manufacturing. India's growth slowed to 4.2 percent in fiscal 2019–20, the lowest in eleven years, before the pandemic hit.

**Regional integration.** RCEP was signed on 15 November 2020 by the ten ASEAN states plus China, Japan, South Korea, Australia and New Zealand. Its members said at signing that they held 2.2 billion people (nearly 30 percent of world population), a combined GDP of roughly $26 trillion and nearly 28 percent of global trade on 2019 data, and it was the first free trade agreement linking China, Japan and South Korea. India withdrew before signing. The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) entered into force on 30 May 2019. By 5 December 2020, 54 African Union members had signed it and 34 had ratified it, and the AU Assembly decided that trading would start on 1 January 2021, although rules of origin and tariff schedules were still being negotiated.

**The 2020 shock.** Lockdowns first hit supply in China (first-quarter output fell 6.8 percent year on year) and then demand worldwide. The oil market went through three steps:
- OPEC+ failed to agree on cuts on 6 March 2020, and Saudi Arabia announced higher output.
- On 12 April OPEC+ agreed a record cut of 9.7 million barrels per day from May.
- On 20 April the expiring May contract for West Texas Intermediate crude closed at −$37.63 per barrel, the first negative price in its history, because storage at Cushing, Oklahoma was nearly full.

Governments and central banks responded with fiscal and monetary support on an unprecedented scale. The IMF's October 2020 Fiscal Monitor projected that global public debt would approach a record 100 percent of GDP, after government measures worth $11.7 trillion (close to 12 percent of global GDP) as of 11 September 2020. Support was very unequal: rich economies could borrow freely, while many developing economies faced capital flight, falling remittances and commodity revenues. The G20 created a Debt Service Suspension Initiative for the poorest countries, and in November 2020 Zambia defaulted on a eurobond payment. The EU agreed a €750 billion recovery instrument (NextGenerationEU) in July 2020, financed by common borrowing.

**Regional figures** (all projections or preliminary estimates at end-2020, subject to revision):
- **World:** IMF October 2020 projection of −4.4 percent for 2020, with a partial rebound to 5.2 percent in 2021. The IMF said all economies except China would stay below 2019 output levels well into 2021.
- **China:** +2.3 percent (preliminary NBS figure, January 2021), with quarterly growth of −6.8, +3.2, +4.9 and +6.5 percent.
- **India:** −23.9 percent year on year in April–June 2020 and −7.5 percent in July–September (MoSPI/NSO first estimates), India's first technical recession on quarterly records going back to 1996. Former chief statistician Pronab Sen and others warned that first estimates probably understated the collapse because informal-sector activity was measured through corporate proxies.
- **Latin America and the Caribbean:** −7.7 percent (ECLAC, December 2020), after earlier ECLAC projections of −5.3 percent (April) and −9.1 percent (July).
- **Sub-Saharan Africa:** −3.3 percent (World Bank Africa's Pulse, October 2020); the pandemic could push up to 40 million Africans into extreme poverty.
- **Global poverty:** 88 to 115 million additional extremely poor people in 2020 and up to 150 million by 2021 (World Bank, October 2020), with extreme poverty defined as living on less than $1.90 a day.

### Social and demographic conditions

**Population.** Humanity numbered about 7.7 to 7.8 billion (UN estimates), most of it in Asia. China and India each had well over a billion people. Population growth was concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia and parts of the Middle East, while East Asia and Europe were ageing. China's seventh census was carried out in 2020, but results were not due until 2021.

**Displacement.** UNHCR's June 2020 Global Trends report counted 79.5 million forcibly displaced people at the end of 2019, up from 70.8 million a year earlier and nearly double the 2010 figure. The total comprised:
- 45.7 million internally displaced people;
- 26.0 million refugees, including 5.6 million Palestine refugees under UNRWA;
- 4.2 million asylum-seekers;
- 3.6 million Venezuelans displaced abroad.

Syria alone accounted for about 13.2 million. New displacement in 2019 was concentrated in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the Sahel, Yemen and Syria. By late 2020, UNHCR and IOM counted about 5.4 million refugees and migrants from Venezuela, 4.6 million of them within Latin America and the Caribbean. More than 860,000 Rohingya refugees remained in the Cox's Bazar settlements in Bangladesh (866,457 registered in 2020, per the Bangladesh government–UNHCR registration). In January 2020 the International Court of Justice ordered provisional measures against Myanmar in the genocide case brought by The Gambia.

**Protest.** 2019 saw an unusual cluster of mass protest movements, mostly driven by local grievances (living costs, corruption, constitutional limits, police conduct) rather than a single ideology:
- the Hirak in Algeria, which forced President Bouteflika to resign in April 2019;
- the Sudanese uprising, which ended in the military removing Omar al-Bashir on 11 April 2019;
- Hong Kong's anti-extradition protests;
- Chile's estallido social, which led to an October 2020 referendum approving a new constitution;
- movements in Lebanon and Iraq;
- protests in Ecuador, Colombia and Bolivia.

In 2020, pandemic restrictions suppressed street protest in many places, while new movements emerged in the United States after the killing of George Floyd, in Belarus after the disputed August 2020 election, in Thailand, in Nigeria (#EndSARS) and among Indian farmers.

**Religious and communal politics.** In India, Hindu nationalist politics produced the Citizenship Amendment Act of December 2019 and protests against it. In China, mass detention of Uyghurs and other Muslims in Xinjiang was contested in scale and nature. Beijing called the facilities vocational training centers. Researcher Adrian Zenz, working from government documents, estimated in 2018 that internment "may be estimated at just over one million" and raised this to up to 1.8 million by November 2019; Chinese Human Rights Defenders estimated 1.1 million.

### Environment and natural events

WMO's provisional December 2020 assessment put the global mean temperature for January to October 2020 at around 1.2 °C above the 1850–1900 baseline, making 2020 very likely one of the three warmest years on record despite a cooling La Niña. The six years from 2015 were the warmest on record, and more than 80 percent of the global ocean experienced a marine heatwave at some point in 2020. WMO said there was at least a one-in-five chance that a single year would temporarily exceed 1.5 °C by 2024. Lockdowns cut carbon dioxide emissions in 2020, but atmospheric concentrations still reached new highs.

Major events included:
- **The desert locust upsurge**, which began in late 2019 and was the worst in decades across Ethiopia, Kenya and Somalia, spreading to Yemen, the Gulf, Iran, Pakistan and India. FAO called it an unprecedented threat to food security, with around 20 million people in six East African countries already acutely food-insecure in early 2020.
- **Australia's 2019–20 bushfires.**
- **Amazon fires in 2019**, which became a diplomatic dispute between Brazil and European governments.
- **Cyclone Idai** (March 2019) in Mozambique, Malawi and Zimbabwe.
- **Cyclone Amphan** (May 2020) in the Bay of Bengal.
- **The 4 August 2020 Beirut port explosion**, a failure of governance rather than nature, which deepened Lebanon's collapse.

**Climate politics.** The United States formally left the Paris Agreement on 4 November 2020, and the incoming administration had pledged to rejoin. In September 2020 China announced it aimed to peak emissions before 2030 and reach carbon neutrality before 2060, and the EU, Japan and South Korea announced 2050 net-zero targets. COP26 was postponed to 2021.

### Flashpoints of the period

1. **The India–China frontier.** Standoffs from May 2020 led to the 15–16 June clash in the Galwan Valley, the first deaths on the border since 1975. India reported 20 soldiers killed; China acknowledged casualties but gave no figure during 2020. Tens of thousands of troops were wintering at high altitude with disengagement unresolved, between two nuclear-armed states.

2. **The Taiwan Strait, South China Sea and Korean Peninsula.** Tsai Ing-wen was re-elected in Taiwan in January 2020, and PLA air activity near Taiwan increased. South China Sea disputes persisted. US–North Korea summits (Singapore, June 2018; Hanoi, February 2019) produced no denuclearization agreement.

3. **The Persian Gulf and the Iran file.** The United States withdrew from the JCPOA in May 2018 and imposed "maximum pressure" sanctions. Then came:
   - attacks on tankers and on Saudi oil facilities at Abqaiq and Khurais (September 2019);
   - the US killing of Qasem Soleimani in Baghdad (3 January 2020), Iranian missile strikes on US bases in Iraq and the Iranian shoot-down of a Ukrainian airliner;
   - the killing of nuclear scientist Mohsen Fakhrizadeh in November 2020.

   The Abraham Accords (from August–September 2020) normalized relations between Israel and the UAE, Bahrain, Sudan and Morocco without settling the Palestinian question.

4. **The Horn of Africa.** The Tigray war began on 3–4 November 2020 when Tigrayan forces attacked federal Northern Command bases; federal and allied forces captured Mekelle on 28 November. Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed declared the operation over, but TPLF leaders said they would fight on. There were credible reports of Eritrean involvement, and a communications blackout limited verification. The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam's first filling in 2020 was disputed by Egypt and Sudan, Sudan's transition was fragile, and locusts added to the pressure.

5. **The post-Soviet space.** The 44-day Nagorno-Karabakh war (27 September to 10 November 2020) ended with a Russian-brokered ceasefire; Azerbaijan regained large territories, including Shusha, and Russian peacekeepers deployed. Azerbaijan reported 2,783 soldiers killed; an Armenian official had confirmed 2,317 dead by mid-November, and final Armenian figures were not yet published. Protests against Prime Minister Pashinyan followed. Belarus's crisis, the poisoning of Alexei Navalny (August 2020) and the frozen war in Ukraine's Donbas remained active.

Other theaters could also escalate:
- Libya (Haftar's 2019–2020 offensive on Tripoli, Turkish intervention, and an October 2020 ceasefire);
- Yemen;
- northern Syria;
- Afghanistan (the US–Taliban Doha agreement of 29 February 2020 and intra-Afghan talks from September);
- the central Sahel;
- Mozambique's Cabo Delgado.

### Emerging forces

- **Rapid vaccine platforms.** mRNA vaccines (Pfizer–BioNTech and Moderna) were authorized in December 2020 after less than a year of development; adenoviral vector vaccines (Oxford–AstraZeneca, Sputnik V) and Chinese inactivated-virus vaccines (Sinopharm, Sinovac) were at or near authorization. Manufacturing and distribution, including through COVAX, were open questions.
- **Machine learning at scale.** OpenAI's GPT-3 (June 2020) and DeepMind's AlphaFold at CASP14 (November 2020) signaled fast capability gains whose economic significance was debated.
- **Space beyond the old powers.** China's Chang'e-4 made the first far-side lunar landing (January 2019), Chang'e-5 returned lunar samples in December 2020, and BeiDou was completed in June 2020. The UAE launched the Hope Mars probe in July 2020, and SpaceX flew the first private crewed orbital flight in May 2020.
- **Continental trade frameworks and energy transition.** AfCFTA and RCEP could change trade patterns if implemented. Solar, wind and battery costs kept falling, with China dominating the supply chains.

### Systemic fragilities

- **Pandemic exposure and health capacity**, including dependence on concentrated supplies of protective equipment and pharmaceutical ingredients.
- **Supply-chain concentration.** Advanced semiconductors depended heavily on Taiwan's TSMC, and US export controls against Huawei in 2019–2020 showed that dependence could be weaponized. Rare earths and battery materials were concentrated in China. The Strait of Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb and the Strait of Malacca remained critical.
- **Debt.** The IMF's October 2020 Fiscal Monitor projected global public debt to approach a record 100 percent of GDP. Many low-income countries were in or near distress, and creditors were fragmented among Chinese state lenders, private bondholders and traditional official creditors.
- **Commodity dependence.** Petro-states (Gulf monarchies, Nigeria, Angola, Algeria, Iraq, Venezuela, Russia) faced fiscal crises when oil collapsed; food importers in the Arab world and Africa were exposed to price shocks.
- **Contested successions and legitimacy.** Belarus; postponed elections in Ethiopia and Hong Kong; and in the United States, the outgoing president disputed the November 2020 election, which Joe Biden won.
- **Multilateral erosion.** The WTO's dispute system was paralysed; the United States announced it would leave WHO (effective July 2021); the INF Treaty ended in 2019; New START was due to expire in February 2021 unless extended.

## III. Major Powers and Regional Blocs

### People's Republic of China

**Internal politics.** Xi Jinping consolidated authority; in March 2018 the National People's Congress removed presidential term limits. COVID-19 was first identified in Wuhan at the end of 2019. Early suppression of information (Dr Li Wenliang was reprimanded for warning colleagues and died of the disease in February 2020) was followed by a lockdown of Wuhan and Hubei on 23 January 2020 and a declaration that the domestic outbreak was controlled by spring; how to judge this record was contested. The Hong Kong National Security Law (30 June 2020) criminalized secession, subversion, terrorism and collusion with foreign forces and overrode local law. The government declared absolute poverty eliminated by its own measure, and in late 2020 halted Ant Group's planned listing, signaling a new approach to platform regulation.

**External relations.** China was in:
- trade confrontation with the United States;
- a frontier clash with India;
- a trade dispute with Australia (after Canberra called for a COVID-origins inquiry);
- South China Sea disputes with several ASEAN states.

It also concluded RCEP and, in December 2020, reached political agreement with the EU on the Comprehensive Agreement on Investment.

**Economic basis.** China was the largest manufacturer and goods exporter and the main buyer of many commodities. In 2020 output passed 100 trillion yuan (NBS preliminary: 101.6 trillion), R&D spending was 2.40 percent of GDP, and retail sales fell 3.9 percent, showing reliance on exports and investment. Outside analysts had long questioned the reliability of Chinese national accounts.

**Knowledge and technique.** Chang'e-4 and Chang'e-5, Tianwen-1 (launched July 2020), BeiDou, the Jiuzhang photonic quantum device (December 2020, a disputed claim), large-scale 5G, and inactivated COVID-19 vaccines (Sinopharm received conditional domestic approval on 31 December 2020). He Jiankui's November 2018 announcement of gene-edited babies was condemned, and he was later sentenced by a Chinese court.

**Key individuals and organizations.**
- People: Premier Li Keqiang; Vice Premier Liu He; Wang Yi and Yang Jiechi; respiratory expert Zhong Nanshan; military vaccine scientist Chen Wei; Jack Ma (Alibaba); Ren Zhengfei (Huawei); Carrie Lam (Hong Kong).
- Organizations: the Communist Party and Central Military Commission; the People's Bank of China; policy lenders (China Development Bank, China Exim Bank); firms such as Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Huawei.

### United States of America

**Internal politics.** The Trump administration (2017 to January 2021) pursued tariff-based trade policy, immigration restriction, tax cuts and judicial appointments, and the president was impeached (December 2019) and acquitted (February 2020). In 2020 the United States recorded more confirmed COVID-19 cases and deaths than any other country in WHO data. Protests after George Floyd's killing in May 2020 were among the largest in the country's history. Congress passed the CARES Act in March 2020 and a further relief package in December. Joe Biden won the November 2020 election; the outgoing president did not concede, and his legal challenges had failed by end-2020. The transfer of power was scheduled for 20 January 2021.

**External relations.** The United States:
- withdrew from the JCPOA (2018), the INF Treaty (2019), the Paris Agreement (effective November 2020) and, by announcement, WHO;
- held summits with North Korea;
- negotiated the Doha agreement with the Taliban;
- brokered the Abraham Accords;
- pressed allies to exclude Huawei;
- sanctioned Venezuela, Iran and Chinese officials over Hong Kong and Xinjiang.

**Economic basis and technique.** The largest economy at market exchange rates, centered on services, technology, finance and energy; shale had made the United States the largest oil producer. The Federal Reserve reopened dollar swap lines to other central banks in March 2020. The United States led in frontier AI, cloud computing, biotechnology (Moderna; Pfizer with Germany's BioNTech) and commercial space (SpaceX), and Operation Warp Speed funded vaccine development at scale.

**Key individuals and organizations.**
- People: Anthony Fauci; Jerome Powell; Robert Lighthizer; Mike Pompeo; Nancy Pelosi; technology executives including Jeff Bezos, Mark Zuckerberg, Tim Cook and Elon Musk.
- Organizations: the Federal Reserve; the Commerce Department (Entity List); the CDC; the large platform firms; pharmaceutical companies.

### Republic of India

**Internal politics.** Narendra Modi's BJP won a larger majority in 2019. The government revoked Jammu and Kashmir's special status and split the state into two union territories (August 2019), with a long communications shutdown. It passed the Citizenship Amendment Act (December 2019), which set off nationwide protests and, in February 2020, deadly communal violence in Delhi. The March 2020 lockdown, among the strictest anywhere, caused a mass return of migrant workers to villages, often on foot. Three agricultural laws passed in September 2020 brought farmers, mainly from Punjab and Haryana, to camp on Delhi's borders from late November.

**External relations.** The Pulwama attack and Balakot airstrikes (February 2019) were the most serious India–Pakistan military exchange in years. After Galwan, India restricted Chinese investment and banned many Chinese apps. It drew closer to the Quad partners (Australia rejoined the Malabar exercise in 2020) and stayed out of RCEP.

**Economic basis and technique.**
- A large IT-services export sector.
- A pharmaceutical industry supplying much of the world's generic medicines and vaccines; the Serum Institute of India was the largest vaccine maker by volume and licensed the AstraZeneca vaccine.
- Agriculture employing a large share of workers.
- Mostly informal employment, which made official statistics hard to interpret.
- Digital public infrastructure (Aadhaar, UPI) and ISRO's programs (Chandrayaan-2, 2019, whose lander failed).

**Key individuals and organizations.**
- People: Amit Shah; Nirmala Sitharaman; S. Jaishankar; Ajit Doval; Shaktikanta Das (Reserve Bank); Adar Poonawalla (Serum Institute); Mukesh Ambani, whose Reliance Jio raised large foreign investment in 2020.
- Organizations: the BJP and Sangh Parivar network; the Reserve Bank; the Election Commission; ISRO; farmers' unions.

### Russian Federation

Constitutional amendments approved in July 2020 allowed Vladimir Putin to seek two more terms. Alexei Navalny was poisoned with a nerve agent in August 2020; Western governments said it was Novichok, and the Kremlin denied involvement. Russia registered Sputnik V in August 2020 before large trials were complete.

Externally, Russia:
- kept forces in Syria;
- supported Haftar in Libya through the Wagner group (not officially acknowledged);
- brokered and garrisoned the Karabakh ceasefire;
- backed Lukashenko;
- deepened alignment with China;
- fought an oil price war with Saudi Arabia before joining the OPEC+ cut.

Its economy rested on hydrocarbons, metals, grain and arms, under Western sanctions since 2014. Key figures included Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin (from January 2020), Sergei Lavrov, Sergei Shoigu and Alexander Novak.

### European Union and European states

The EU dealt with Brexit (the UK left on 31 January 2020, and a trade and cooperation agreement was reached on 24 December 2020), a new Commission under Ursula von der Leyen, rule-of-law disputes with Hungary and Poland, and a severe early pandemic in Italy and Spain. NextGenerationEU introduced large-scale joint borrowing. Angela Merkel was in her final term, and Emmanuel Macron pressed for European "strategic autonomy." The EU called China a "partner, competitor and systemic rival" (2019) and managed eastern Mediterranean tensions with Turkey. Its strengths were the single market, regulatory power (GDPR from 2018), advanced manufacturing and pharmaceuticals; BioNTech's mRNA vaccine was the first authorized in the West (UK, 2 December 2020).

### Japan, the Korean Peninsula and Southeast Asia

**Japan.** Shinzo Abe resigned for health reasons in August 2020 and Yoshihide Suga succeeded him; the Tokyo Olympics were postponed to 2021; Japan signed RCEP and kept the CPTPP going.

**Korean Peninsula.** Moon Jae-in's engagement with the North stalled after Hanoi, and in June 2020 North Korea blew up the inter-Korean liaison office. South Korea's early mass testing made it a widely cited COVID model.

**Southeast Asia.** ASEAN, chaired by Vietnam in 2020, concluded RCEP.
- **Vietnam** gained manufacturing as firms diversified from China and kept reported COVID cases very low.
- **Indonesia**, the fourth most populous country, re-elected Joko Widodo in 2019 and passed a contested omnibus job-creation law in 2020.
- **Myanmar's** NLD won the November 2020 election by a wide margin and the military-aligned party disputed the result; the military's intentions were a live concern but not a settled fact.
- **Thailand** faced youth-led reform protests.
- **The Philippines** under Rodrigo Duterte balanced Washington and Beijing.

### The Arab world, Iran, Turkey and Israel

**Saudi Arabia and the Gulf.** Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman concentrated power; the murder of Jamal Khashoggi in Istanbul (October 2018) damaged Riyadh's standing; Saudi Aramco listed in December 2019; the Yemen war and attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure continued. The Qatar embargo lasted through 2020 with signs of a settlement at year's end. The UAE projected power in Yemen, Libya and the Horn and normalized relations with Israel.

**Iran.** "Maximum pressure" sanctions caused contraction and high inflation. According to Amnesty International, suppression of fuel-price protests killed at least 304 people between 15 and 18 November 2019; Iran called such figures lies and had issued no official death toll by mid-2020. Iran was an early COVID-19 hotspot, and after the Soleimani and Fakhrizadeh killings it expanded nuclear activity beyond JCPOA limits in steps.

**Turkey.** Under Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Turkey:
- intervened in northern Syria (October 2019) and Libya, where its support turned back Haftar's offensive in 2020;
- backed Azerbaijan in the Karabakh war, including with Bayraktar TB2 drones;
- contested maritime zones with Greece and Cyprus;
- faced a 2018 currency crisis and further lira weakness in 2020.

**Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Israel.** Iraq's October 2019 Tishreen protests brought down a government. In Syria, the Assad government regained most populated territory with Russian and Iranian support, but Idlib and the Kurdish-held northeast stayed outside its control. Lebanon's financial collapse from late 2019 and the port explosion made it one of the period's most severe crises. Israel held three inconclusive elections (April 2019, September 2019, March 2020) before an unstable unity government that was collapsing at the end of 2020.

### Africa

**Nigeria**, Africa's most populous country, faced the jihadist insurgency in the northeast (Boko Haram and ISWAP), rural banditry, farmer-herder violence, oil-revenue losses and the #EndSARS protests. Those protests ended after the Lekki toll gate shooting in Lagos (20 October 2020), whose facts were contested and which was then under judicial investigation.

**Ethiopia.** Abiy Ahmed became prime minister in 2018, made peace with Eritrea, won the 2019 Nobel Peace Prize and replaced the EPRDF coalition with the Prosperity Party, which the TPLF refused to join. The postponement of the 2020 elections, Tigray's own election in September 2020 and the war from November followed.

**South Africa** was hit hard by COVID-19, and the 501Y.V2 variant was identified there in December 2020. Cyril Ramaphosa chaired the African Union in 2020 and led its pandemic response.

**Sudan.** After Bashir's fall a Transitional Military Council ruled. The June 2019 massacre of protesters in Khartoum preceded the constitutional declaration of 17 August 2019, which created a Sovereignty Council chaired by General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan and a civilian government under Prime Minister Abdalla Hamdok for a 39-month transition. The Juba peace agreement followed (October 2020), and the United States removed Sudan from its state-sponsors-of-terrorism list in December 2020. Power was split between the army, the Rapid Support Forces under Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo ("Hemedti") and civilian parties.

**The Sahel and elsewhere.**
- Jihadist insurgencies linked to al-Qaeda (JNIM) and the Islamic State in the Greater Sahara spread across Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger. Mali had a military coup in August 2020. France's Operation Barkhane and the UN mission MINUSMA were the main external forces.
- The DRC's Félix Tshisekedi came to power through a disputed 2018–2019 election.
- A jihadist insurgency grew in Mozambique's Cabo Delgado near major gas projects.
- The Africa CDC, led by John Nkengasong, coordinated pandemic responses.

### Latin America and the Caribbean

**Brazil.** Jair Bolsonaro took office in January 2019, reduced environmental enforcement and played down COVID-19; Brazil's reported deaths were second only to the United States by end-2020.

**Venezuela.** Nicolás Maduro began a second term in January 2019 after a 2018 election many governments did not recognize. National Assembly president Juan Guaidó declared himself interim president on 23 January 2019, and more than 50 countries recognized him, including the United States, Canada, most EU states and many Latin American governments. Russia, China, Cuba, Turkey and others backed Maduro, who kept control of the state and armed forces. Hyperinflation, collapsing oil output and US sanctions deepened the humanitarian crisis, and about 5.4 million Venezuelans were abroad by late 2020. December 2020 National Assembly elections, boycotted by the main opposition, gave Maduro's camp the legislature, leaving Guaidó's claim legally uncertain.

**Elsewhere.**
- Mexico's Andrés Manuel López Obrador (from December 2018) prioritized energy nationalism and social programs.
- Chile voted in October 2020 to write a new constitution.
- In Bolivia, Evo Morales resigned and left after a disputed 2019 election, and Luis Arce of his party won the October 2020 election; whether the 2019 events were a coup was contested.
- Peru had three presidents in one week in November 2020.
- Argentina restructured its foreign bond debt in 2020.

Latin America was the most unequal region and one of the worst-hit by COVID-19.

## IV. Thematic Sections

### Knowledge, science and technology

Chinese scientists published the SARS-CoV-2 genome in January 2020, and vaccine development began worldwide almost at once. By December 2020:
- the UK, US and other regulators had authorized the Pfizer–BioNTech vaccine, and the US had authorized Moderna's;
- China had given conditional approval to Sinopharm, which the UAE and Bahrain had also authorized;
- Russia had registered Sputnik V;
- the Oxford–AstraZeneca vaccine, licensed for manufacture by the Serum Institute of India, was approved in the UK on 30 December 2020.

The UK RECOVERY trial's dexamethasone result (June 2020) became standard care for severe cases. Several national research and industrial traditions (American, German, British, Chinese, Russian and Indian) produced vaccines against the same pathogen at the same time.

Deep learning dominated AI research, with GPT-3 and AlphaFold as 2020 landmarks and Chinese AI output expanding fast. Semiconductors became geopolitical: US controls cut Huawei off from advanced chipmaking in 2020, and China made chip self-sufficiency a national priority.

### Health and population

**COVID-19 timeline.**
- 31 December 2019: pneumonia cases in Wuhan were reported to WHO.
- January 2020: a novel coronavirus was identified and human-to-human transmission confirmed.
- 30 January 2020: WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern.
- 11 March 2020: WHO characterized the outbreak as a pandemic.

WHO's update of 5 January 2021 (data to 3 January) reported more than 83 million confirmed cases and more than 1.8 million deaths, with more than 4 million new cases a week for the third week running. WHO counts included only confirmed cases, and the undercount varied greatly by country. In December 2020 new variants were reported from the United Kingdom (VOC-202012/01) and South Africa (501Y.V2); by 5 January 2021 the UK variant had been detected in 40 other countries or territories.

**Responses varied widely, with no consensus on what worked best:**
- elimination-style border and quarantine regimes (China, Taiwan, Vietnam, New Zealand, Australia);
- mass testing and tracing (South Korea);
- strict national lockdowns (India, Italy, Spain, France, the UK, Peru);
- looser voluntary approaches (Sweden);
- minimization by national leadership (Brazil, in part the United States, and Tanzania, which stopped publishing case data in 2020).

**Other health crises.** The 2018–2020 Ebola outbreak in eastern DRC, the second-largest on record, was fought in a conflict zone with the first large use of an approved Ebola vaccine and declared over in June 2020. Yemen's cholera epidemic and African swine fever in China's pig herd (from 2018) also mattered, and pandemic disruption of immunization and of tuberculosis, HIV and malaria programs was feared to carry large indirect death tolls.

### Security and conflict

The Karabakh war was the most intense interstate war of the period, notable for Azerbaijan's use of Turkish and Israeli drones and loitering munitions against Armenian armor and air defenses. India and China fought a deadly border clash without firearms, because agreements limited their use; India and Pakistan exchanged airstrikes in 2019; the United States and Iran traded strikes in 2019–2020.

Civil and internationalized wars continued in:
- Yemen;
- Syria;
- Libya (a proxy war involving Turkey, the UAE, Egypt and Russian contractors, with a UN-backed ceasefire in October 2020);
- Afghanistan;
- the Sahel and Lake Chad;
- Somalia;
- eastern DRC;
- Cameroon's Anglophone regions;
- Cabo Delgado;
- from November 2020, Tigray.

In most theaters casualty counts were contested, with partisan official figures and independent estimates (ACLED, the Uppsala Conflict Data Program) that later research often revised.

World military spending grew for five straight years from 2015 to 2019 (SIPRI). In 2019 the Americas accounted for 43 percent, Asia and Oceania 27 percent, Europe 19 percent and Africa 2.1 percent; South East Asian spending rose 4.2 percent to $40.5 billion.

### Culture and society

The pandemic pushed life into digital channels. Remote work, schooling and e-commerce grew sharply: by NBS data, China's online retail sales rose 14.8 percent in 2020 while total retail sales fell. School closures widened inequality where connectivity was poor. ByteDance's Douyin/TikTok became a global cultural channel, and South Korean popular culture (BTS; *Parasite*, which won the 2020 Academy Award for Best Picture) reached very large audiences. The 2020 Hajj was limited to a small number of pilgrims already in Saudi Arabia. The Hagia Sophia was reconverted to a mosque in July 2020, and in November 2019 India's Supreme Court ruled on the Ayodhya dispute, allowing a temple on the site of the demolished Babri mosque.

## V. Conclusion

At the end of 2020 the world had just been through a synchronized emergency. On the IMF's October projection global output was shrinking by more than 4 percent, the World Bank expected up to 115 million more people in extreme poverty, and more than 1.8 million confirmed deaths had been recorded, a count known to understate the toll. Beneath the emergency lay three structural features of 2018–2020:
- the US–China rivalry reorganizing trade, technology and alliances;
- Asia's growing weight as the demographic and manufacturing center and as a builder of its own trade institutions;
- visibly weaker multilateral institutions alongside more assertive middle powers (Turkey, the Gulf states, India, Russia, Ethiopia) in their own regions.

**Open questions at end-2020:**
- **The pandemic.** Would the new vaccines work at population scale, how fast could they be distributed, and would the new variants weaken them? COVAX's ability to supply poorer countries was untested.
- **Unequal recovery.** China was already growing again, rich economies had borrowed heavily, and many developing economies faced debt distress with little fiscal room.
- **US direction.** A new administration promised to rejoin the Paris Agreement and WHO and to consult allies. Whether US–China competition would ease or harden, and whether allies would align or hedge, was unresolved.
- **Active conflicts.** Tigray, the India–China standoff, post-war Karabakh, Afghanistan after the US drawdown, Libya's ceasefire, Yemen and the Sahel were all live.
- **Contested transitions.** Sudan, Myanmar's disputed election result, Belarus, Venezuela's rival claims to authority and Hong Kong under the new law each had more than one plausible path.
- **Climate.** Major emitters made mid-century neutrality pledges for the first time; whether they would become policy was untested ahead of COP26.

An informed observer at the time could reasonably have expected either a fast vaccine-led recovery or a long, uneven one, and either managed great-power competition or a sharper split.
