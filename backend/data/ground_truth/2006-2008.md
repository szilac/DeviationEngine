# Ground-Truth World-State Report: 2006 to 2008

## I. Executive Summary

The years 2006 to 2008 opened in the fourth and fifth years of the fastest, most geographically broad expansion of world output since the early 1970s, and closed in a financial and trade contraction whose depth was not yet known to anyone living through it. For most of the period, informed contemporaries in finance ministries, central banks and international organisations described a world whose growth engine had moved to Asia and to commodity exporters. China, India and Russia together were estimated by the IMF to have produced about half of global growth in the year to mid-2007. Commodity-exporting regions, including much of sub-Saharan Africa, the Gulf, Russia and South America, were experiencing their strongest terms of trade in a generation.

**Political developments.** The period's major political developments were distributed across the world rather than concentrated in one region:
- **Russia.** A resource-financed Russian state rebuilt reserves and openly contested the post-1991 security order, culminating in the August 2008 war with Georgia.
- **China.** The Chinese party-state staged the Beijing Olympics as a statement of national standing while facing the Lhasa unrest and the Wenchuan earthquake in the same spring.
- **South America.** Left-of-centre governments, sometimes grouped under the label "pink tide," consolidated power and founded UNASUR (May 2008) as a South American body without the United States.
- **Middle East and Horn of Africa.**
  - Hamas won Palestinian elections (January 2006) and then took Gaza (June 2007).
  - Israel and Hezbollah fought an inconclusive war (July–August 2006).
  - Iraq passed through its most lethal sectarian phase before violence fell from late 2007.
  - Ethiopia intervened in Somalia (December 2006).
- **South Asia.** India pursued and in September 2008 obtained a Nuclear Suppliers Group waiver. Pakistan passed through the Lal Masjid siege, a state of emergency, Benazir Bhutto's assassination, and Musharraf's resignation. Nepal abolished its monarchy.

**Economy.** Economically, the defining shocks were price shocks:
- **Oil.** Crude oil rose to a nominal record above $147 a barrel in July 2008, then fell steeply.
- **Food.** A food-price surge in 2007–08, amplified by export restrictions, produced protests or riots in several dozen countries.
- **Hyperinflation.** Zimbabwe's official annual inflation reached 231 million per cent in July 2008, after which official statistics effectively stopped.
- **Credit.** From August 2007 a credit contraction originating in United States mortgage securities spread through wholesale funding markets. Contemporaries called it the "subprime crisis" or "credit crunch" and widely expected it to be contained. Estimates of losses were revised sharply upward through 2008. After mid-September 2008, with the failure of Lehman Brothers and the rescues that followed, it became a general financial crisis centred on the North Atlantic.

**Knowledge and technique.** Notable advances in knowledge and technique came from many places:
- Chinese, Japanese and Indian lunar orbiters (2007–08) and China's first spacewalk (September 2008).
- Human induced pluripotent stem cells (Japan and the US, 2007).
- The iPhone (2007) and the announcement of Android (2007).
- Kenya's mobile-money service M-Pesa (2007).
- The IPCC Fourth Assessment Report (2007).
- The start-up of CERN's Large Hadron Collider (September 2008).

**Humanitarian disasters and epidemics.** Major humanitarian disasters were concentrated in Asia and Africa:
- Cyclone Nargis in Myanmar (May 2008) left about 138,000 dead or missing.
- The Wenchuan earthquake in Sichuan (May 2008) left about 69,000 dead and nearly 18,000 missing.
- Darfur's conflict continued, with death estimates disputed by a factor of thirty.
- Kenya's post-election violence (2007–08) killed more than 1,100 people.
- Zimbabwe's cholera epidemic began in late 2008.

**Overall characterisation.** The world of 2006–2008 was one of unusually strong but unevenly shared growth. Economic and diplomatic weight was visibly redistributing toward large Asian states and commodity producers. The period was also marked by an overextended United States military commitment in Iraq and Afghanistan, and by multiple unresolved regional wars and nuclear standoffs. Until the second half of 2008, the dominant contemporary uncertainty was whether high commodity prices, inflation and a North Atlantic credit contraction would end the expansion. It was not yet known how far any of them would.

## II. Global Overview

### Distribution of power

The period's genuine great powers and regional hegemons, by contemporary measures of military reach, fiscal capacity and economic weight, are described below.

**The United States.** The US remained the only state with global military reach and the issuer of the principal reserve currency, while heavily committed in Iraq and Afghanistan.

**The European Union and its large members.** The EU enlarged to 27 members in January 2007. Its largest members were Germany, France and the United Kingdom, whose foreign policies diverged on Russia, Kosovo and Iraq.

**China.** China was ruled by the Communist Party under Hu Jintao and Wen Jiabao, whose official programme emphasised a "harmonious society" and a "scientific outlook on development."

**Japan.** Japan was the second-largest economy at market exchange rates, but politically unsettled: four prime ministers held office between 2006 and 2008.

**Russia.** Russia under Vladimir Putin as president (to May 2008) and then as prime minister under President Dmitry Medvedev was fiscally strengthened by energy revenue.

**India.** India under the Congress-led United Progressive Alliance government of Manmohan Singh grew at around nine per cent a year and negotiated a civil nuclear agreement with the United States.

**Regional powers.**
- Iran projected influence through Iraq, Lebanon and Gaza while under UN Security Council sanctions over enrichment.
- Saudi Arabia and the Gulf monarchies were flush with oil revenue.
- Turkey under the AKP government of Recep Tayyip Erdoğan was re-elected in 2007 and survived a constitutional closure case in 2008.
- Brazil under Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva played a coordinating role in South America.
- South Africa and Nigeria were the continental economic anchors in sub-Saharan Africa.
- Ethiopia was the leading military actor in the Horn.
- Indonesia had consolidated its post-1998 democracy.

**Main lines of tension.** The main lines of relationship and tension ran along several axes:
- Between Russia and the United States-led NATO over enlargement, missile defence and Kosovo.
- Between the United States and Iran across the Gulf, Iraq and Lebanon.
- Between the two Palestinian governments after June 2007.
- Between India and Pakistan, whose composite dialogue continued until the Mumbai attacks of November 2008.
- Between Colombia and its neighbours Ecuador and Venezuela in the Andes.

**Lines of cooperation.** Lines of cooperation included the Six-Party Talks on North Korea and the Forum on China–Africa Cooperation, whose November 2006 Beijing summit was the largest diplomatic gathering China had hosted. They also included new South American and South–South groupings: UNASUR, the Banco del Sur initiative, and the first meeting of BRIC foreign ministers in Yekaterinburg in May 2008.

### Economy and exchange

National accounts existed for almost all states, but the figures of the period were revised repeatedly, sometimes substantially. Growth figures below should be read as estimates subject to later revision.

**Global growth and the PPP revision.** The IMF's view of global growth changed not only because the world changed but because the measurement changed. In October 2007 the IMF put world growth at 5.4 per cent for 2006 and 5.2 per cent for 2007. After the International Comparison Program released new purchasing-power-parity weights in December 2007, the same institution restated world growth at 5.0 per cent for 2006 and 4.9 per cent for 2007. It also projected 3.7 per cent for 2008 in April of that year. The downward shift came mainly from the new PPP benchmark, which reduced the measured size of fast-growing economies such as China and India relative to earlier estimates.

**China.**
- The National Bureau of Statistics first reported 11.4 per cent growth for 2007 (February 2008), revised it to 11.9 per cent (April 2008), and then to 13.0 per cent in January 2009. The January 2009 revision was described at the time as final.
- Nominal GDP benchmark revisions after the 2008 economic census raised the 2007 level further, so later official series differ again.
- The 2007 communiqué recorded consumer-price inflation of 4.8 per cent, with food prices up 12.3 per cent.

**India.** India's Central Statistical Organisation estimated real growth of 9.6 per cent in fiscal 2006–07 and 9.0 per cent in 2007–08. Foodgrain output reached a record 230.7 million tonnes in 2007–08.

**Russia.** Russia's gross international reserves peaked at roughly $597–598 billion in early August 2008. The Stabilization Fund built from oil taxes was split in February 2008 into a Reserve Fund and a National Welfare Fund.

**Advanced economies.** United States growth slowed from 2.9 per cent in 2006 to about 2 per cent in 2007 as residential investment fell. The euro area grew above its recent trend in 2006–07. Japan grew modestly.

**Trade networks.** Value moved through long and dense supply networks. East Asian assembly networks centred on the Pearl River and Yangtze deltas imported components from Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Southeast Asia and exported finished goods to North America and Europe. Raw materials moved from Australia, Brazil, Chile, Peru, Russia, the Gulf and Africa to China and the other industrial economies.

**Balances and capital flows.**
- The large United States current-account deficit was mirrored by surpluses in China, Japan, Germany and the oil exporters. Economists at the time called this pattern "global imbalances," and whether it was sustainable was a central policy debate.
- State investment vehicles attracted attention as holders of these surpluses. The China Investment Corporation was established in 2007, and Gulf and Singaporean sovereign funds recapitalised several Western banks in late 2007 and early 2008. These "sovereign wealth funds" were then widely debated as a new force in global finance.
- South Asian firms made large cross-border acquisitions. Tata Steel acquired the Anglo-Dutch Corus in 2007, and Tata Motors acquired Jaguar and Land Rover in 2008. Mittal Steel's 2006 takeover of Arcelor created the largest steelmaker.

**Price shocks.**
- **Oil.** Crude rose through 2007, passed $100 a barrel at the start of 2008, and reached an intraday record of $147.27 on the New York Mercantile Exchange on 11 July 2008. It then fell to the low-to-mid $30s by late December 2008.
- **Food.** The FAO food price index peaked in June 2008. The FAO reported that the cereal import bill of the poorest countries was forecast to rise by more than half in 2007–08, and by about three-quarters for low-income food-deficit countries in Africa. Rice prices rose most sharply after major exporters restricted sales. The causes were contested at the time: biofuel mandates, oil and fertiliser costs, drought in grain exporters, low stocks, speculative positions in futures markets and export restrictions each had advocates.
- **Zimbabwe.** Zimbabwe's official Central Statistical Office reported annual inflation of 231 million per cent for July 2008. Independent estimates diverged by many orders of magnitude, from tens of billions of per cent to the Johns Hopkins economist Steve Hanke's peak estimate of about 89.7 sextillion per cent for mid-November 2008.

**The credit contraction.** Losses on United States mortgage-linked securities surfaced as a liquidity freeze in August 2007, followed by bank rescues in Britain (Northern Rock) and the United States (Bear Stearns, March 2008). Contemporaneous loss estimates grew with each assessment: the IMF put declared losses on US-originated loans and securities at $945 billion in April 2008 and about $1.4 trillion in October 2008. In September–October 2008 came several events in quick succession:
- the federal takeover of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac;
- Lehman Brothers' bankruptcy (15 September 2008);
- the rescue of AIG;
- bank failures and nationalisations in Europe;
- Iceland's banking collapse.

Coordinated central-bank action and national rescue packages followed, as did China's announcement of a four-trillion-yuan stimulus programme (November 2008) and the first summit of G20 leaders in Washington (November 2008). By then, contemporaries were describing a global financial crisis, but its depth and duration were unknown.

### Social and demographic conditions

**Population and urbanisation.** World population was estimated by the United Nations at about 6.6–6.7 billion. The UN projected that during 2008 the urban share of humanity would pass one-half for the first time. The threshold depends on national definitions of "urban," which vary widely, so the date is a statistical convention rather than a measured event.

**Internal migration in China.** China's internal migrant population was usually estimated at well over 100 million people. The estimates vary with definitions, and figures from 130 to 150 million were common.

**Forced displacement.** Forced displacement on a large scale came from several conflicts:
- **Iraq.** Iraqis fled to Syria and Jordan in numbers that host governments put at well over a million each. UN agencies used figures around two million outside the country, and later surveys suggested lower totals, so the figure is contested.
- **Darfur.** Displacement exceeded 2.5 million by 2008.
- **Somalia.** The US Congressional Research Service estimated about 1.1 million people displaced after the Ethiopian intervention.
- **Kenya.** The post-election violence displaced at least 300,000 people.

**Migration toward Europe and South Africa.** Irregular maritime migration from West Africa to Spain's Canary Islands peaked in 2006. Migration across the Mediterranean from Libya and Tunisia continued. In South Africa, attacks on foreign nationals in May 2008 killed dozens and displaced tens of thousands.

**Religious and political currents.**
- Political Islam was a significant electoral and social force: Hamas in Palestine, the AKP in Turkey, the Muslim Brotherhood's electoral presence in Egypt, and the Islamic Courts in Somalia.
- Buddhist monks led the September 2007 protests in Myanmar.
- In Latin America, movements drawing on indigenous and labour constituencies were in government in Bolivia and Ecuador and influential elsewhere.
- In China, a large internet-using population took shape.

**Subsistence pressures.** Food-price inflation eroded real incomes for the urban poor across Africa, South Asia and the Caribbean in 2007–08, and the FAO revised its count of undernourished people upward.

### Environment and natural events

**Disasters.**
- **Cyclone Nargis** struck Myanmar's Ayeyarwady delta on 2 May 2008 and left about 138,000 people dead or missing. The Myanmar authorities initially restricted foreign relief.
- **The Wenchuan earthquake** of 12 May 2008 killed about 69,000 people in Sichuan and neighbouring provinces, left about 17,900 missing, and destroyed millions of buildings. Collapsed school buildings became a matter of public grievance.
- **Cyclone Sidr** struck Bangladesh in November 2007.
- **Severe winter storms** in southern China in January–February 2008 disrupted transport during the Spring Festival migration.
- **Drought** affected grain production in Australia and parts of the Black Sea region, and contributed to the tightness of global grain markets.

**Climate science and diplomacy.** The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change published its Fourth Assessment Report in 2007, stating that warming was unequivocal and very likely mostly human-caused. The IPCC shared the 2007 Nobel Peace Prize with Al Gore. Arctic summer sea-ice extent reached a record minimum in September 2007. The Bali climate conference in December 2007 agreed a negotiating "roadmap" toward a successor to the Kyoto Protocol, due to conclude at Copenhagen in 2009.

**Epidemics.**
- The HIV epidemic remained the largest infectious-disease burden. Its measured size was revised sharply downward in 2007 (see Health and population).
- Avian influenza H5N1 caused sporadic human cases, especially in Indonesia, Egypt, Vietnam and China, and was the pandemic threat for which most national preparedness plans were written.
- Chikungunya spread across Indian Ocean islands and India in 2006.
- Zimbabwe's cholera epidemic began in August 2008 and spread widely by December.

### Flashpoints of the period

The following were the zones with the highest potential for sudden conflict or drastic change, as they appeared in 2006–2008:

- **The Gulf and Iran's enrichment programme.** UN Security Council sanctions (from December 2006) and United States–Iranian confrontation in Iraq set the context. Recurrent speculation about Israeli or US military action and the exposure of the Strait of Hormuz made this the zone most often named as a potential oil-price catastrophe.
- **The Caucasus and the NATO–Russia frontier.** NATO's April 2008 Bucharest declaration that Georgia and Ukraine would become members, Kosovo's February 2008 declaration of independence (which Russia opposed), and unresolved conflicts in Abkhazia and South Ossetia converged in the August 2008 war. After it, the security status of Ukraine, Crimea's Russian naval base and Georgia remained open questions.
- **The Pakistan–Afghanistan frontier and India–Pakistan relations.** The Taliban insurgency in Afghanistan was expanding. Militancy was spreading into Pakistan's tribal areas and Swat, and Pakistani civilian rule was fragile after Musharraf. The Mumbai attacks of 26–29 November 2008 put two nuclear-armed states into acute tension at the end of the period.
- **The Levant.** Gaza was under Hamas and blockaded, the West Bank under Fatah. Lebanon endured an 18-month political deadlock, broken by street fighting in Beirut and the Doha Agreement of May 2008. Negotiations launched at Annapolis in November 2007 had not concluded. Israel began large-scale operations in Gaza on 27 December 2008.
- **The Horn of Africa and Sudan.** Somalia's transitional government lost control of most of the south to insurgents. Piracy grew in the Gulf of Aden, a principal Europe–Asia shipping route. In Sudan, Darfur's conflict, the fragile 2005 North–South peace agreement, and the International Criminal Court prosecutor's request for an arrest warrant for President Omar al-Bashir (July 2008) were all live.

Secondary zones of tension included the Korean peninsula after North Korea's October 2006 nuclear test. They also included the Andean triangle after Colombia's March 2008 raid on a FARC camp in Ecuador, and Thailand's recurring confrontations between supporters and opponents of the ousted prime minister Thaksin Shinawatra.

### Emerging forces

The following were small or new in 2006–2008 but were discussed by contemporaries as capable of large growth. This section does not assume which of them grew.

- **Mobile telephony in low-income countries,** and on it, mobile money. M-Pesa was launched in Kenya in 2007 and adopted rapidly by people without bank accounts.
- **Touchscreen smartphones and mobile internet access,** following the iPhone (2007) and the announcement of the open-source Android platform (2007; first handset October 2008). In China, a large share of internet users already connected via mobile phones by mid-2008.
- **Social networking and user-generated platforms** (Facebook opened to the general public in 2006; Twitter launched in 2006; YouTube was acquired by Google in 2006), alongside virtual worlds such as Second Life, which attracted heavy corporate and media interest in 2006–07.
- **Sovereign wealth funds and state-owned investors** from China, the Gulf, Singapore and Russia, seen at the time as potentially dominant new holders of global assets.
- **South–South financial and political institutions:** the China–Africa Development Fund (2007), UNASUR (2008), the proposed Banco del Sur, and BRIC ministerial meetings.
- **Biofuels,** promoted by the United States, the European Union and Brazil as an energy-security and climate measure, and by 2008 also blamed for food-price inflation.
- **Human induced pluripotent stem-cell techniques** (2007), which offered a route around embryonic-stem-cell controversies.
- **A peer-to-peer electronic cash design** published on 31 October 2008 under the name Satoshi Nakamoto. It was entirely obscure at the time.

### Systemic fragilities

- **Dependence on short-term wholesale funding** and on complex securitised credit in North Atlantic banking. Losses in one asset class could freeze interbank lending worldwide, as August 2007 and September 2008 showed.
- **"Global imbalances":** a large US external deficit financed by surplus economies, with widespread concern at the time about a disorderly fall of the dollar.
- **Concentrated oil supply and chokepoints** (Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb and the Gulf of Aden, Malacca), with little spare production capacity during 2007–08.
- **Low world grain stocks,** combined with the readiness of exporters to restrict sales, which transmitted price spikes to import-dependent countries.
- **Heavy reliance on commodity revenue** in Russia, the Gulf, Venezuela, Nigeria, Angola and others, exposing budgets to price reversals.
- **Gas transit through Ukraine to the EU,** interrupted briefly in January 2006 during a Russian–Ukrainian price dispute.
- **Fragile power-sharing and successions:**
  - the unity government negotiated in Kenya (February 2008);
  - the Zimbabwean agreement of September 2008;
  - Pakistan's transition after Musharraf;
  - Lebanon's Doha arrangement;
  - Thailand's disputed politics after the September 2006 coup;
  - the governments of Iraq and Afghanistan dependent on foreign troops.
- **Epidemic exposure:** the anticipated H5N1 pandemic, and water-system collapse in Zimbabwe.

## III. Major Powers and Regional Blocs

### China (People's Republic)

**Internal politics.**
- The 17th Party Congress (October 2007) confirmed Hu Jintao and elevated Xi Jinping and Li Keqiang to the Politburo Standing Committee, positioning them as likely successors.
- Official doctrine emphasised the "scientific outlook on development" and a "harmonious society," addressing inequality, rural poverty and environmental damage.
- March 2008 brought the largest unrest in Tibetan areas in two decades.
  - The official toll of those killed by rioters in Lhasa was 18 civilians and one police officer.
  - The Tibetan government-in-exile's count of Tibetans killed across the region reached about 140 by the end of March.
  - The figures could not be independently verified because of access restrictions.
- In September 2008, melamine contamination of infant formula became a national scandal.

**External relations.**
- The November 2006 FOCAC Beijing summit brought delegations from 48 African states. China pledged to double aid by 2009 and to provide $3 billion in preferential loans and $2 billion in buyer's credits, and it established a China–Africa development fund intended to reach $5 billion.
- Relations with Taiwan eased after Ma Ying-jeou's election (March 2008), and regular direct cross-strait flights began in December 2008.
- China held the chair of the Six-Party Talks on North Korea.

**Economic basis.**
- Export manufacturing and very high investment rates in infrastructure, housing and heavy industry.
- Official foreign-exchange reserves approaching two trillion dollars by 2008.
- Rapid growth in domestic consumption and internet use: the China Internet Network Information Center reported 253 million users at mid-2008, overtaking the United States. Penetration was about 19 per cent, below the world average.

**Knowledge and technique.**
- The Chang'e-1 lunar orbiter (October 2007).
- The Shenzhou 7 mission with China's first spacewalk (September 2008).
- The Beijing–Tianjin intercity high-speed railway (August 2008).
- Large-scale expansion of higher education and research spending.

**Key individuals beyond Hu Jintao.**
- Premier Wen Jiabao, the public face of disaster response in 2008.
- Zhou Xiaochuan, governor of the People's Bank of China.
- Wang Qishan (vice-premier from 2008).
- Xi Jinping and Li Keqiang.
- The artist Ai Weiwei, a consultant on the Olympic stadium and later a critic of the handling of the Sichuan school collapses.
- The 14th Dalai Lama, in exile in Dharamsala, whose "Middle Way" proposals were the reference point of Tibetan politics.

**Key organisations.**
- The Communist Party's Central Committee and Politburo.
- The State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission and the large state enterprises it oversaw (in oil, telecoms and construction).
- The China Investment Corporation (2007).
- The China Development Bank and the Export-Import Bank of China.
- The Beijing Organizing Committee for the Olympic Games.

### India

**Internal politics.**
- The Congress-led United Progressive Alliance governed with outside support from the Left parties until July 2008. The Left withdrew over the civil nuclear agreement with the United States, and the government then survived a confidence vote.
- Communal and caste politics, Maoist (Naxalite) insurgency in central and eastern states, and bombings in several cities in 2008 were major internal concerns.

**External relations.**
- The India–US civil nuclear initiative proceeded through the US Hyde Act (2006) and an IAEA safeguards agreement. In September 2008 the Nuclear Suppliers Group granted an exception. The Indian government presented it as consistent with national consensus, while opponents at home and some abroad saw it as undermining non-proliferation norms.
- The composite dialogue with Pakistan continued until the Mumbai attacks of 26–29 November 2008, carried out by gunmen who arrived by sea.

**Economic basis.**
- Services, especially software and IT-enabled services, grew at double-digit rates.
- Manufacturing expanded, and domestic saving rose to about 35 per cent of GDP.
- A strong 2007 monsoon supported record foodgrain output.
- Large Indian business groups became outward investors, notably the Tata group's acquisitions of Corus and of Jaguar Land Rover.

**Knowledge and technique.**
- Chandrayaan-1, India's first lunar mission (October 2008).
- The unveiling of the very low-cost Tata Nano car (January 2008).
- Continued growth of generic pharmaceutical production supplying much of the developing world's antiretroviral drugs.

**Key individuals beyond Manmohan Singh.**
- Sonia Gandhi, president of the Congress party.
- Finance Minister P. Chidambaram.
- Prakash Karat, general secretary of the CPI(M), who led the Left's opposition to the nuclear deal.
- Ratan Tata.
- Mukesh and Anil Ambani.
- Mayawati, chief minister of Uttar Pradesh from 2007.

**Key organisations.**
- The Reserve Bank of India.
- The Indian Space Research Organisation.
- The Tata group, Reliance Industries, Infosys and Wipro.
- The Left Front parties.

### Russia (Russian Federation)

**Internal politics.**
- Power was centralised in the presidency.
- The theoretical label "sovereign democracy," associated with the Kremlin official Vladislav Surkov, was promoted from 2006.
- Dmitry Medvedev was elected president in March 2008, with Vladimir Putin becoming prime minister, a "tandem" whose internal balance was debated.

**External relations.**
- In his Munich Security Conference speech (February 2007), Putin denounced a "unipolar" world and NATO enlargement.
- Russia suspended participation in the Conventional Armed Forces in Europe Treaty (2007).
- It opposed Kosovo's independence and blocked Security Council endorsement of the Ahtisaari plan.
- In August 2008 Russia fought a five-day war with Georgia after Georgian forces shelled Tskhinvali on the night of 7–8 August, and on 26 August recognised Abkhazia and South Ossetia.
- Accounts of the war diverged from the start. Russia described its actions as defence of its peacekeepers and of South Ossetia's population. Georgia described a long-prepared Russian aggression.
- The EU-commissioned Tagliavini fact-finding mission reported in 2009, after the period. It found that the Georgian shelling began the large-scale armed conflict and was not justified under international law. It also found that Russia's response went far beyond reasonable self-defence and violated international law, and that the war was the culmination of years of mutual provocation. Both governments claimed vindication.

**Economic basis.**
- Oil, gas and metals exports.
- Rapid growth of consumption and bank credit.
- Accumulated reserves, which peaked in August 2008, and sovereign funds built under Finance Minister Alexei Kudrin.
- Russian equity markets fell steeply in the autumn of 2008.

**Knowledge and technique.**
- A continued space-launch role, including crew transport to the International Space Station.
- Nuclear-power exports through Rosatom.

**Key individuals beyond the president.**
- Alexei Kudrin.
- Igor Sechin (deputy prime minister overseeing energy from 2008).
- Alexei Miller of Gazprom.
- Sergei Lavrov (foreign minister).
- Vladislav Surkov.
- Opposition figures and critics, including Garry Kasparov and the journalist Anna Politkovskaya, who was murdered in October 2006.

**Key organisations.**
- Gazprom, Rosneft and Transneft.
- The Federal Security Service.
- The United Russia party.
- The Collective Security Treaty Organization and the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, whose other members included China and the Central Asian states.

### The United States

**Internal politics.**
- The Democratic Party won both houses of Congress in November 2006.
- Barack Obama won the presidential election on 4 November 2008. The long Democratic primary contest with Hillary Clinton and the general election against John McCain framed domestic debate in 2008.
- The housing downturn and financial rescues, including the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program enacted in October 2008, dominated the final months of the period.

**External relations.**
- The "surge" of additional troops into Iraq was announced in January 2007, under General David Petraeus.
- Counter-insurgency in Afghanistan continued, as did the confrontation with Iran over enrichment.
- The United States was a leading sponsor of the Annapolis process on Israel–Palestine and of the India nuclear initiative. It recognised Kosovo in February 2008.

**Economic basis.**
- The largest economy at market exchange rates, with the dominant financial centre.
- The dollar was the principal reserve and invoicing currency.
- The economy was dependent on foreign financing of its external deficit.

**Knowledge and technique.**
- Leading research universities and firms.
- The iPhone and Android.
- Genome sequencing technologies whose cost was falling rapidly.
- Contributions to human iPS cell research.

**Key individuals beyond President George W. Bush.**
- Federal Reserve Chairman Ben Bernanke.
- Treasury Secretary Henry Paulson.
- Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice.
- Defense Secretary Robert Gates (from December 2006).
- General David Petraeus.

**Key organisations.**
- The Federal Reserve.
- The Treasury.
- The large investment banks: Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Bear Stearns, Lehman Brothers and Merrill Lynch.
- The government-sponsored mortgage enterprises Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.
- Google and Apple.

### The European Union and its member states

**Internal politics.**
- Enlargement to 27 members (Bulgaria and Romania, January 2007).
- The Lisbon Treaty was signed in December 2007 to replace the rejected Constitutional Treaty. Irish voters rejected it in June 2008, leaving ratification unresolved.
- National leadership changed in France (Nicolas Sarkozy, May 2007) and the United Kingdom (Gordon Brown, June 2007). Germany was governed by a grand coalition under Angela Merkel.

**External relations.**
- The EU's French presidency brokered the Russia–Georgia ceasefire in August 2008.
- On Kosovo, 22 of 27 member states recognised independence by the end of 2008. Spain, Romania, Slovakia, Greece and Cyprus did not, citing international law and their own secession concerns. An EU rule-of-law mission, EULEX, was launched.
- Energy dependence on Russian gas divided members' approaches to Moscow.

**Economic basis.**
- A large single market and a euro area of 15 members by 2008 (Slovenia 2007; Cyprus and Malta 2008).
- Strong German export manufacturing.
- Construction booms in Spain and Ireland.
- Fast-growing Central and Eastern European economies financed partly by foreign-currency lending.
- Several European banks held large exposures to US mortgage securities.

**Knowledge and technique.**
- CERN's Large Hadron Collider began circulating beams on 10 September 2008 but was halted by a magnet incident nine days later.
- Emission-trading and renewable-energy policy were intended as international models.

**Key individuals.**
- José Manuel Barroso (Commission president).
- Javier Solana (foreign-policy representative).
- Jean-Claude Trichet (European Central Bank).
- Martti Ahtisaari, UN envoy on Kosovo's status and 2008 Nobel Peace laureate.

**Key organisations.**
- The European Commission and Council.
- The European Central Bank.
- NATO, with which most EU members were allied.

### Japan

**Internal politics.**
- Koizumi Jun'ichirō left office in September 2006. Abe Shinzō, Fukuda Yasuo and Asō Tarō followed in quick succession, each serving about a year.
- The opposition Democratic Party of Japan under Ozawa Ichirō won the upper house in July 2007, producing a divided parliament.
- A scandal over lost pension records damaged the governing Liberal Democratic Party.

**External relations.**
- The alliance with the United States.
- Participation in the Six-Party Talks, with the abduction of Japanese nationals by North Korea as a domestic priority.
- A thaw with China after Abe's 2006 visit to Beijing.

**Economic basis.**
- Advanced manufacturing (automobiles and electronics) and component exports into East Asian production networks.
- A weak yen supported exports until 2008.
- Growth was modest and deflationary pressure persistent.

**Knowledge and technique.**
- Yamanaka Shin'ya's derivation of induced pluripotent stem cells from mouse (2006) and human (2007) cells.
- The Kaguya lunar orbiter (2007).

**Key organisations.**
- The Liberal Democratic and Democratic parties.
- The Bank of Japan.
- Toyota, the most profitable automaker of the period.

### Iran, the Arab states, Turkey and Israel

**Iran.**
- Under President Mahmoud Ahmadinejad (and the Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei), Iran expanded uranium enrichment in defiance of UN Security Council resolutions beginning with Resolution 1737 (December 2006).
- It supported allied movements in Iraq and Lebanon.
- It faced high inflation despite oil revenue.

**Iraq.**
- Iraq's elected government under Nouri al-Maliki faced a sectarian civil war that intensified after the February 2006 bombing of the al-Askari shrine in Samarra.
- Saddam Hussein was executed in December 2006.
- Violence fell from late 2007, attributed variously to the US troop increase, the Sunni tribal "Awakening" movements, the Sadrist ceasefire, and the prior sectarian separation of Baghdad's neighbourhoods. The weight of each factor was debated then and remains contested.
- Estimates of Iraqi deaths from March 2003 to mid-2006 varied widely:
  - The media-based Iraq Body Count registered about 47,000 civilian deaths in that window.
  - The WHO–Iraqi Ministry of Health household survey (published January 2008) estimated about 151,000 violent deaths, with an uncertainty range of roughly 104,000 to 223,000.
  - The Johns Hopkins survey published in The Lancet (October 2006) estimated about 655,000 excess deaths, with a range of about 426,000 to 794,000.

**Israel and Palestine.**
- Hamas won the Palestinian legislative election of January 2006. After an unrecognised unity government, Hamas took control of Gaza in June 2007, and the Fatah-led Palestinian Authority governed in the West Bank.
- Israel's war with Hezbollah (July–August 2006) killed more than 1,000 people in Lebanon, mostly civilians, and about 160 Israelis, mostly soldiers, and displaced about a million people in Lebanon.
- Israel's official Winograd Commission (final report January 2008) called the war a serious missed opportunity with grave failings in decision-making. Hezbollah and much Arab commentary presented the outcome as a victory for the resistance.
- Talks relaunched at Annapolis in November 2007 were continuing when Israel began large-scale operations in Gaza on 27 December 2008.

**Lebanon.** Lebanon was deadlocked between the March 14 and March 8 coalitions over the presidency, until fighting in Beirut in May 2008 and the Qatari-mediated Doha Agreement.

**Saudi Arabia and the Gulf.**
- King Abdullah's government used oil revenue for large domestic programmes and sponsored Arab mediation, including the February 2007 Mecca agreement between Fatah and Hamas.
- Gulf states' sovereign funds invested abroad on a large scale.
- Dubai's property and construction boom drew labour from South Asia.

**Turkey.** Turkey's governing AKP was re-elected in July 2007. Abdullah Gül became president after a confrontation with the military over the presidency, and the party survived a constitutional court closure case in July 2008.

**Egypt.** Egypt under Hosni Mubarak faced labour protests, including the Mahalla textile strikes of 2006–08, and bread shortages in 2008.

**Key organisations.** Hezbollah and Hamas; the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps; the Arab League; the Organisation of the Islamic Conference; OPEC; Al Jazeera, whose Arabic channel shaped regional coverage.

### Pakistan and Afghanistan

**Pakistan.**
- The military government of Pervez Musharraf tried to dismiss the Chief Justice, Iftikhar Chaudhry, in March 2007, which provoked a lawyers' movement.
- It stormed the Lal Masjid in Islamabad in July 2007, and a state of emergency followed in November 2007.
- Benazir Bhutto returned from exile, survived the Karachi bombing of October 2007, and was assassinated at a rally in Rawalpindi on 27 December 2007.
- Elections in February 2008 brought the Pakistan People's Party to office. Musharraf resigned in August 2008, and Asif Ali Zardari became president in September 2008.
- Militancy spread in the Federally Administered Tribal Areas and Swat, and the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan was formed in late 2007.

**Afghanistan.**
- The government of Hamid Karzai depended on NATO's International Security Assistance Force.
- The Taliban insurgency intensified in the south and east.
- Opium production reached record levels in 2007.

### Southeast Asia

**Indonesia.** Indonesia under Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono consolidated democracy and decentralisation. The Aceh peace (2005) held and Aceh's first local elections were held in 2006. Indonesia had the largest number of human H5N1 cases in the period.

**Thailand.** The military overthrew Thaksin Shinawatra in September 2006. Parties allied with him won the December 2007 election. The People's Alliance for Democracy occupied Government House and, in late November 2008, Bangkok's airports, before a court dissolved the governing party in December 2008.

**Myanmar.** Myanmar's military government under Than Shwe suppressed monk-led protests in September 2007. It held a constitutional referendum in May 2008, days after Cyclone Nargis. Aung San Suu Kyi remained under house arrest.

**Vietnam.** Vietnam joined the World Trade Organization in January 2007 and grew rapidly, but faced high inflation in 2008.

**Regional organisation.** ASEAN adopted its Charter in November 2007.

### Sub-Saharan Africa

**Growth and new partners.** Growth was strong in commodity exporters and in several diversified economies. The IMF expected African growth of about 6 per cent in 2007 and projected higher for 2008. China's presence deepened through loans, infrastructure contracts and resource investments, a relationship Chinese officials described as partnership without political conditions and critics described in terms of resource extraction and support for unaccountable governments. African governments generally welcomed it as widening their options. The United States created a unified military command for Africa (AFRICOM) in 2007–08.

**South Africa.** Jacob Zuma defeated Thabo Mbeki for the ANC presidency at Polokwane in December 2007. The ANC recalled Mbeki from the state presidency in September 2008, and Kgalema Motlanthe served as interim president. Electricity shortages from early 2008 and xenophobic violence in May 2008 marked domestic politics.

**Nigeria.** Umaru Musa Yar'Adua won the April 2007 election, which observers widely criticised. Militancy in the Niger Delta repeatedly disrupted oil output.

**Kenya.**
- The 27 December 2007 presidential election was disputed. An official review later concluded it was impossible to tell who had won.
- Violence in January–February 2008 killed at least 1,133 people according to the Waki Commission. The Kenya National Commission on Human Rights counted 1,162, and many observers put the total nearer 1,500. At least 300,000 people were displaced.
- The African Union panel led by Kofi Annan mediated a power-sharing agreement in February 2008 that made Raila Odinga prime minister under President Mwai Kibaki.

**Zimbabwe.** The March 2008 elections were followed by violence and the opposition leader Morgan Tsvangirai's withdrawal from the June runoff. A power-sharing agreement mediated by South Africa was signed in September 2008, with implementation stalled through the end of the year amid hyperinflation and cholera.

**Sudan.**
- In Sudan, as in Darfur, figures carried political weight. In April 2008 the UN humanitarian chief John Holmes described up to 300,000 deaths from violence, disease and hunger since 2003 as a "reasonable extrapolation," not a new survey result.
- The Sudanese government put deaths at about 10,000, counting only combat deaths, and rejected the UN figure.
- Some independent analysts argued the UN figure was an undercount.
- A joint UN–AU force (UNAMID) was authorised in 2007 but deployed slowly.

**The Horn of Africa.**
- The Islamic Courts Union held Mogadishu from June 2006 until Ethiopian forces, supporting the Transitional Federal Government, took the city in December 2006.
- An insurgency followed, including the al-Shabaab movement.
- The African Union mission AMISOM deployed from 2007.
- A June 2008 Djibouti agreement between the government and part of the opposition provided for Ethiopian withdrawal, which began at the end of 2008.

**Other conflicts.** Eastern Democratic Republic of Congo saw renewed fighting between the army and Laurent Nkunda's CNDP, reaching the outskirts of Goma in late 2008. Chadian rebels reached N'Djamena in February 2008.

**Key individuals and organisations.**
- Meles Zenawi (Ethiopia).
- Ellen Johnson Sirleaf (Liberia, inaugurated January 2006, Africa's first elected woman head of state).
- Kofi Annan as mediator.
- Jean Ping (African Union Commission chair from 2008).
- Muhammad Yunus's Grameen model (Nobel Peace Prize 2006), influential across African microfinance.
- The African Union, the Southern African Development Community, and IGAD.
- Safaricom (M-Pesa).

### Latin America

**Governments in power.** Governments of the left or centre-left held office across much of South America:
- Lula in Brazil;
- Hugo Chávez in Venezuela, re-elected in December 2006 but defeated in a constitutional-reform referendum in December 2007;
- Evo Morales in Bolivia (from January 2006);
- Rafael Correa in Ecuador (from January 2007);
- Néstor Kirchner and then Cristina Fernández de Kirchner in Argentina (from December 2007);
- Michelle Bachelet in Chile (from March 2006);
- Tabaré Vázquez in Uruguay;
- Fernando Lugo in Paraguay (from August 2008);
- Daniel Ortega in Nicaragua (from January 2007).

These governments differed considerably, from market-oriented social democracy to Venezuela's "socialism of the twenty-first century" and the Venezuelan-led ALBA bloc.

**Colombia and Mexico.** Colombia under Álvaro Uribe pressed a military campaign against the FARC, including the March 2008 raid in Ecuador that killed the FARC commander Raúl Reyes and triggered a regional crisis, and the July 2008 rescue of Ingrid Betancourt and other hostages. Mexico under Felipe Calderón (from December 2006) deployed the military against drug-trafficking organisations, and violence rose sharply.

**UNASUR and Bolivia.** UNASUR's founding treaty was signed in Brasília on 23 May 2008. In September 2008, after violence in Bolivia's Pando department killed at least 16 people, UNASUR presidents met in Santiago. They backed the Morales government against what they called attempts at a civil coup. Spanish and Chilean analysts described the meeting as South America solving its own problems without the United States or the OAS, while noting rivalry between Brazilian and Venezuelan leadership.

**Economic basis.** The region's economy rested on commodity exports (soy, iron ore, oil, copper and meat) with strong demand from China. Brazil announced large offshore "pre-salt" oil discoveries in 2007–08.

**Key organisations.** Petrobras and PDVSA, Mercosur, UNASUR, ALBA and the Banco del Sur initiative.

## IV. Thematic Sections

### Knowledge, science and technology

**Space.** Space exploration had several national centres:
- China's Chang'e-1 (2007) and Shenzhou 7 (2008);
- Japan's Kaguya (2007);
- India's Chandrayaan-1 (2008);
- the US Phoenix Mars lander (2008).

The International Astronomical Union's 2006 definition of a planet reclassified Pluto.

**Physics and biology.**
- CERN's Large Hadron Collider started operation in September 2008 before an accident halted it.
- The Japanese laboratory of Yamanaka Shin'ya and, in parallel, US groups derived human induced pluripotent stem cells in 2007.
- Sequencing costs fell steeply as new instruments arrived.

**Information technology.**
- The touchscreen smartphone arrived (iPhone 2007) and an open mobile operating system was announced (Android 2007).
- E-readers appeared (Kindle 2007).
- Social platforms spread rapidly.
- The largest national internet population shifted from the United States to China in 2008.
- In East Africa, mobile money gave millions without bank accounts access to transfers.

**Engineering and industry.**
- The Beijing–Tianjin high-speed railway.
- The Tata Nano low-cost car.
- Very large infrastructure programmes in China, the Gulf and India.
- Rapid expansion of wind and solar manufacturing, especially in Germany, China, Spain and Denmark.

**Climate science and agricultural research.** The IPCC Fourth Assessment Report (2007) was the period's authoritative scientific synthesis on climate. Agricultural research institutes, including the International Rice Research Institute, returned to prominence in 2008 as food prices raised questions about stagnant yield growth.

### Health and population

**HIV.** In November 2007, UNAIDS and the WHO revised the estimated number of people living with HIV from 39.5 million (2006) to 33.2 million, a reduction of 16 per cent. Most of the change came from methodology rather than a decline in the epidemic. A national household survey in India, with more than 100,000 people tested, reduced India's estimate from 5.7 million to 2.5 million. Revisions in Angola, Kenya, Mozambique, Nigeria and Zimbabwe accounted for much of the rest, though some genuine declines in new infections were reported in Kenya, Zimbabwe and Thailand. Antiretroviral treatment in low- and middle-income countries was expanding with support from the Global Fund, PEPFAR and Indian generic production.

**Infectious disease.**
- Avian influenza H5N1 remained the principal pandemic concern.
- Malaria control expanded through insecticide-treated nets.
- Cholera in Zimbabwe (from August 2008) reached hundreds of deaths by early December. Rights groups estimated higher figures than the WHO, while President Mugabe asserted that the outbreak had been contained.

**Food safety.** In China, melamine-adulterated infant formula sickened a very large number of infants in 2008 and caused several deaths, according to official figures.

**Demographic pressures.**
- Ageing in Japan, Europe and China, with Japan's population having begun to decline.
- Very young and fast-growing populations in sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia and the Middle East.
- Continued urbanisation in China and India.
- Sex-ratio imbalances at birth in China and parts of India.

### Security and conflict

**Major armed conflicts.** The period's major armed conflicts included:
- the Iraq war and its sectarian civil conflict;
- the Afghan insurgency;
- the Israel–Hezbollah war (2006) and the Gaza confrontation;
- Darfur and the Chad–Sudan proxy conflict;
- Somalia after the Ethiopian intervention;
- eastern Congo;
- Sri Lanka, where war with the LTTE resumed in 2006 and government offensives took the Eastern Province in 2007 and advanced in the north in 2008;
- the Russo-Georgian war (August 2008);
- Mexico's escalating conflict with drug-trafficking organisations.

**Terrorist attacks and insurgency.** Terrorist attacks with mass casualties occurred in Iraq, Pakistan, Afghanistan, India (including Mumbai), Algeria and elsewhere.

**Proliferation.** North Korea conducted its first nuclear test on 9 October 2006. The Six-Party Talks produced a February 2007 agreement and partial disablement at Yongbyon before stalling over verification. Iran's enrichment continued under successive UN sanctions resolutions. India obtained the NSG exception in September 2008.

**Military technique.** The US Army and Marine Corps revised counter-insurgency doctrine (December 2006) and applied it in Iraq. Armed unmanned aircraft were increasingly used in Pakistan's tribal areas. Cyberattacks accompanied the Estonian crisis of 2007 and the Georgian war of 2008. Maritime piracy off Somalia prompted naval deployments by the EU, NATO, and others including India and China by the end of 2008.

**Mediation.** Mediation and peacekeeping by non-Western actors were prominent:
- the African Union in Kenya and Somalia;
- Qatar in Lebanon;
- Saudi Arabia between Palestinian factions;
- UNASUR in Bolivia;
- South Africa in Zimbabwe;
- Egypt between Israel and Hamas.

### Culture and society

**Sport and spectacle.** The Beijing Olympics (August 2008), with its opening ceremony directed by Zhang Yimou, were the period's largest global spectacle. The FIFA World Cup in Germany (2006) and the Indian Premier League (first season 2008) reflected the commercial reach of sport.

**Film and literature.** Film production was multipolar: Hindi, Nigerian (Nollywood), Chinese, Korean and Latin American industries grew alongside Hollywood. Literary Nobel Prizes went to Orhan Pamuk (2006), Doris Lessing (2007) and J. M. G. Le Clézio (2008).

**Religion and political identity.** Religious movements shaped politics:
- Pentecostal and charismatic Christianity grew in Africa, Latin America and parts of Asia.
- Political Islam was a major electoral force.
- Buddhist monks led protest in Myanmar and Tibet.

Indigenous political identity was central in Bolivia's new constitutional process and Ecuador's 2008 constitution, which recognised rights of nature.

**Contested values.** Social values were contested around the role of the state in markets, which became urgent after September 2008. They were also contested around migration in Europe and South Africa, internet freedom and censorship in China, gender and family law in several Muslim-majority states, and the rights and representation of sexual minorities across many societies.

## V. Conclusion

Seen as it stood, the world of 2006–2008 was not organised around a single centre. A fiscally strong and increasingly outward-facing China, a fast-growing India, and a resource-rich Russia willing to use force sat alongside a United States still pre-eminent in military and financial reach, but stretched by two wars and exposed through its credit markets. The European Union was large but politically divided. Commodity producers in the Gulf, Africa and South America enjoyed unusual bargaining power and experimented with new regional institutions.

The live tensions at the close of 2008 were the following:
- whether the North Atlantic financial collapse would become a prolonged world depression, and how far emerging economies could shield themselves;
- whether oil and food prices would stay low or spike again;
- whether Iran's programme, the Georgian aftermath, and the post-Mumbai standoff between India and Pakistan would escalate;
- whether fragile power-sharing arrangements in Kenya, Zimbabwe, Lebanon, Pakistan and Iraq would hold;
- whether the new United States administration, the G20 leaders' format, China's stimulus, and South–South institutions would alter the rules of international cooperation.

Contemporaries could see all of these as open questions, and they were.
