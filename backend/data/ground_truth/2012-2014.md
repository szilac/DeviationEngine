# Ground-Truth World-State Report: 2012 to 2014

## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Major political shifts.** Leadership changed across much of the world's population in these three years:
- China: the 18th Party Congress in November 2012 made Xi Jinping General Secretary, and he became state president in March 2013, with Li Keqiang as premier.
- Japan: Shinzō Abe returned in December 2012.
- South Korea: Park Geun-hye took office in 2013.
- Russia: Vladimir Putin returned to the presidency in May 2012.
- Iran: Hassan Rouhani was elected in 2013.
- India: the 2014 general election gave Narendra Modi's BJP 282 of 543 seats on about 31 percent of the vote, with turnout of about 66.4 percent. It was the first single-party majority since 1984.
- Indonesia: the 2014 election made Joko Widodo president with 53.15 percent against Prabowo Subianto (General Elections Commission, KPU, July 2014).
- Egypt: an elected president, Mohamed Morsi, was removed by the military in July 2013. Abdel Fattah al-Sisi was elected president in 2014.
- Thailand: the army took power in May 2014.
- Ukraine: the Euromaidan protests toppled Viktor Yanukovych in February 2014. Russia then annexed Crimea and war broke out in the Donbas.

**Economic changes.** The post-2008 recovery was slow and uneven. The IMF's October 2014 World Economic Outlook (WEO), subtitled "Legacies, Clouds, Uncertainties", cut its 2014 world growth forecast to 3.3 percent, 0.4 points below its April forecast. The IMF said emerging and developing economies were still supplying "more than two-thirds of global growth" (April 2014 WEO). Other major economic facts of the period:
- The euro crisis eased after the European Central Bank (ECB) pledged in July 2012 to do "whatever it takes".
- The US Federal Reserve's signal of tapering in 2013 shook emerging-market currencies.
- China's official growth slowed from double digits to about 7–8 percent.
- Brent crude peaked at $115.06 on 19 June 2014 and settled at $57.33 on 31 December (US Energy Information Administration, EIA).

**Social developments.** Mass urban protest was a defining form of politics. Examples include Brazil (June 2013), Turkey's Gezi Park (2013), Ukraine's Maidan (2013–14), Hong Kong's Occupy Central / Umbrella movement (2014), Thailand's anti-government protests (2013–14), and the Delhi protests after the December 2012 gang-rape. Smartphones and messaging platforms spread across the Global South and changed how such movements organised.

**Advances in knowledge.** Notable results included:
- CERN's announcement of a Higgs-like boson (July 2012).
- The first CRISPR-Cas9 genome-editing papers (2012 onward).
- China's Chang'e-3 lunar landing with the Yutu rover (December 2013).
- India's Mars Orbiter Mission reaching Mars orbit (September 2014).
- The European Space Agency's Rosetta/Philae comet landing (November 2014).
- The IPCC Fifth Assessment Report (2013–14).

**Crises.** The largest were:
- The Syrian war. The UN human rights office documented 191,369 killings to April 2014 and called this "probably an underestimate".
- The Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) offensive in Iraq from June 2014.
- The West African Ebola epidemic.
- Typhoon Haiyan (November 2013).
- The Rana Plaza factory collapse in Bangladesh (April 2013).

**Overall characterization.** In December 2014 the world was more crowded with competing centres than a decade earlier. Economic weight was moving east and south, but the dollar-based financial system and US military reach were intact. Armed conflict and displacement were rising, and several large shocks (oil, Ukraine, Ebola, Iraq–Syria) were still unfolding.

## II. GLOBAL OVERVIEW

### Distribution of power

**The main powers.**
- **United States.** Still the only power with global military reach. SIPRI estimated US military spending at $610 billion in 2014, about a third of the world total of $1,776 billion. It was falling in real terms under the 2011 Budget Control Act. In SIPRI's words, "the third consecutive year of falling global spending".
- **China.** The second-largest military spender, with SIPRI's estimate at about $216 billion in 2014. That figure is an estimate, because China's official defence budget excludes some items.
- **Russia and Saudi Arabia.** Increased military spending substantially. Saudi spending rose 17 percent in 2014, the fastest of any top-15 spender.
- **European Union.** Heavy economic weight but slow, rule-bound collective decisions.

**Regional powers and blocs.** India, Japan, Brazil, Turkey, Iran, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia, Nigeria and South Africa each shaped their regions. BRICS agreed a New Development Bank in July 2014, and China launched the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) initiative in the same year. At the time these were read as efforts to build parallel institutions beside the IMF and World Bank. Whether they would work was an open question.

**Zones of tension.**
- The East and South China Seas: the Senkaku/Diaoyu dispute from 2012, China's air defence identification zone in November 2013, the Scarborough Shoal standoff in 2012, and the Philippines' arbitration filing in January 2013.
- The Russia–West relationship after Crimea.
- The Saudi–Iranian rivalry across Syria, Iraq, Bahrain, Lebanon and Yemen.

**Zones of cooperation.**
- The Iran nuclear talks, which produced the interim Joint Plan of Action (JPOA) in November 2013.
- The WTO's Bali package (December 2013).
- The US–China climate announcement (November 2014).

**Non-state actors that mattered.**
- The Islamic State of Iraq, renamed ISIL/ISIS in April 2013. It declared a "caliphate" as the "Islamic State" on 29 June 2014.
- Jabhat al-Nusra.
- The Syrian Kurdish YPG.
- Boko Haram.
- Al-Shabaab.
- The Houthis (Ansar Allah), who entered Sanaa in September 2014.
- The Afghan Taliban.
- The FARC, in talks with the Colombian government in Havana from 2012.
- The Donetsk and Luhansk "people's republics".

Large technology platforms, state-owned enterprises and sovereign wealth funds operated with reach that crossed borders. So did the Catholic Church under Pope Francis, elected in March 2013.

### Economy and exchange

**Measurement.** The ICP released its 2011 results on 29 April 2014. It estimated world GDP at $90.6 trillion at PPP, against $70.3 trillion at market exchange rates. The World Bank said "almost half of the world's total output came from low and middle income countries."
- **Contested finding.** The revision raised China's PPP GDP by about 20 percent. A Chinese analysis in *China-US Focus* put China at 87 percent of US output in 2011. PIIE's Arvind Subramanian said the US was only 15 percent larger.
- **Media reading.** The *Financial Times* and others reported that China was "poised" to pass the US in 2014.
- **IMF figures.** Using the new PPPs, the IMF's October 2014 database projected China's 2014 PPP GDP at 1.1 percent above the US (as summarised by the US Congressional Research Service).
- **Other measures.** At market exchange rates, the same database put 2013 GDP at about $16.8 trillion for the US and $9.5 trillion for China.
- **Pushback.** Chinese commentators argued that the PPP method overstated China's size.

The fair reading is that the two economies were of comparable size at PPP and far apart at market rates. What counted as "largest" depended on the measure.

**Nigeria's rebasing.** In April 2014 Nigeria's National Bureau of Statistics moved its GDP base year from 1990 to 2010. Estimated 2013 GDP rose from about $285.6 billion to $509.9 billion, an 89 percent increase, making Nigeria Africa's largest economy on paper, ahead of South Africa's $370 billion. The rebased figures showed services at 51 percent of GDP and oil and mining at 20 percent. The Nigeria Labour Congress warned that the new figures had "no effect on welfare". This is a clear case where the reported size of an economy changed through measurement rather than production.

**Production and trade.**
- China was the largest manufacturer. China Daily, citing China's Ministry of Commerce and WTO data, reported that China's merchandise trade in 2013 totalled $4.16 trillion ($2.21 trillion exports, $1.95 trillion imports). That was about $250 billion more than the $3.91 trillion US total reported by the US Commerce Department, making China the largest goods trader by this measure.
- The Gulf, Russia, Nigeria, Angola, Venezuela and others depended on oil.
- Brazil, Australia, Chile, Peru and Indonesia depended on minerals and farm exports sold largely into Chinese demand.
- Chokepoints included the Strait of Hormuz, the Malacca Strait, Bab el-Mandeb and the Suez Canal.

**Disruptions.**
- The 2013 "taper tantrum".
- Western sanctions on Russia from March 2014 and Russian counter-sanctions on food imports from August. The ruble fell steeply in December 2014.
- OPEC's decision on 27 November 2014 to keep its 30 million barrels per day target. EIA said Brent fell more than 10 percent afterwards.
- Argentina's default in July 2014, after US court rulings in favour of holdout creditors.

**Contemporary views on the oil fall.** Analysts attributed it to weaker demand, rising US shale supply and OPEC's stance, and they disagreed about how much weight each factor carried. Forecasts made in December 2014 mostly expected a partial recovery. Nobody knew how long low prices would last.

### Social and demographic conditions

**Population.** The UN's World Population Prospects 2012 Revision (published June 2013) estimated 7.2 billion people in mid-2013 and projected 9.6 billion by 2050. That projection was higher than the 2010 Revision's, mainly because fertility in Africa had been underestimated. China and India together held more than a third of humanity. The UN projected India would pass China around 2028; later revisions changed that date. More than half of future growth was projected to be in Africa, and Europe's population was projected to decline.

**Forced displacement.** UNHCR's Global Trends counted:
- 51.2 million forcibly displaced at end-2013.
- 59.5 million at end-2014: 19.5 million refugees, 38.2 million internally displaced people (IDPs) and 1.8 million asylum-seekers.
- For Syria alone: 7.6 million IDPs and 3.88 million refugees.
- Turkey became the largest refugee host in 2014, with 1.59 million Syrians.
- 219,000 people crossed the Mediterranean in 2014, a record.

**Religious and cultural life.**
- The election of Pope Francis moved the Catholic Church's attention towards the Global South.
- Sectarian polarisation between Sunni and Shia deepened across the Levant and Gulf.
- Salafi-jihadist mobilisation drew foreign fighters to Syria.
- Debates over women's safety (India), labour conditions (Bangladesh) and inequality (Brazil, South Africa, and the reception of Piketty's *Capital in the Twenty-First Century* in 2014) were prominent.

### Environment and natural events

- **Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda), 8 November 2013.** The Philippines' National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC) reported 6,300 dead, 1,061 missing and 28,689 injured, with damage of about ₱89.6 billion. Local accounts suggested a higher death toll.
- **Other disasters.** Hurricane Sandy struck the Caribbean and US east coast in 2012. Northern China suffered severe air-pollution episodes in early 2013, which made pollution a domestic political issue.
- **Climate diplomacy.** The IPCC's Fifth Assessment Report was published in 2013–14. The US and China made a joint emissions announcement in November 2014, and the Lima conference (COP20) in December 2014 produced a framework for national pledges ahead of 2015 talks.
- **Epidemics.**
  - MERS coronavirus was identified in Saudi Arabia in 2012.
  - H7N9 avian influenza appeared in China in 2013.
  - WHO declared polio a public health emergency of international concern (PHEIC) in May 2014.
  - Ebola was confirmed in Guinea in March 2014 and declared a PHEIC in August. By WHO's 31 December 2014 situation report there were 20,206 confirmed, probable and suspected cases and 7,905 reported deaths. Countries with cases included Guinea, Liberia, Sierra Leone, Mali, Nigeria, Senegal, Spain, the US and the UK.

### Flashpoints (as seen in late 2014)

1. **Iraq–Syria.** A self-declared caliphate was fighting several states and militias. US-led airstrikes had begun in Iraq in August and in Syria in September 2014. How far ISIL would get was unknown.
2. **Ukraine–Russia.** The September 2014 Minsk ceasefire was fragile. The UN human rights office recorded at least 1,357 deaths after it took effect, within a total of at least 4,707 killed from mid-April to 12 December 2014.
3. **The East and South China Seas.** Maritime and air incidents were growing more frequent among China, Japan, Vietnam and the Philippines, and there were no reliable crisis-management mechanisms.
4. **Libya and Yemen.** Both had rival authorities by late 2014, with the Houthis in Sanaa and competing parliaments and governments in Libya.
5. **The Korean peninsula.** North Korea had conducted a third nuclear test in February 2013, and Jang Song-thaek was executed in December 2013.

### Emerging forces (as a contemporary might judge)

Each of these was small or new, and its future was uncertain at the time:
- **Deep neural networks.** A deep network won the 2012 ImageNet contest by a wide margin. This was a specialist event in computing, and it was not yet clear how widely the technique would spread.
- **CRISPR genome editing.**
- **Mobile money.** Kenya's M-Pesa was the leading example, and models were spreading across Africa and South Asia.
- **Bitcoin and cryptocurrencies.** Mt. Gox, the largest exchange, collapsed in February 2014.
- **Chinese internet platforms.** Examples were WeChat and Alibaba, which listed in New York in September 2014 in a record IPO.
- **US shale oil and gas.**
- **Cheap solar power.**
- **Chinese state-led connectivity proposals.** These were the "Silk Road Economic Belt" (announced September 2013 in Kazakhstan) and the "21st-Century Maritime Silk Road" (October 2013, Indonesia).
- **The AIIB and the BRICS bank.**
- **Anti-establishment parties in Europe.** Examples were Syriza, Podemos (founded 2014), the Front National and UKIP. These did well in the May 2014 European Parliament elections, and a UK referendum on EU membership had been promised in 2013.

### Systemic fragilities

- **Euro-area weaknesses.** Bank–sovereign links and high unemployment in southern Europe persisted, though banking union was under way.
- **China's debt.** Credit growth, local-government debt and shadow banking raised concerns about a hard landing. These were widely debated, including in Chinese financial media such as Caixin.
- **Commodity dependence.** Exporters from Venezuela and Russia to Nigeria and Angola were exposed when prices fell.
- **Emerging-market dollar debt.** Borrowing in dollars exposed these economies to changes in US interest rates.
- **Fragile successions and transitions.** These included Venezuela after Hugo Chávez's death (March 2013), Afghanistan's disputed 2014 election and the end of the NATO-led ISAF mission, Egypt, Libya, Thailand and South Sudan.
- **Weak health systems.** Ebola showed how quickly these could fail in densely populated cities.

## III. MAJOR POWERS AND REGIONAL BLOCS

### China

**Internal politics.** The 18th Party Congress elevated a seven-member Politburo Standing Committee under Xi Jinping. Key figures included Li Keqiang, Zhang Dejiang, Yu Zhengsheng, Liu Yunshan, Wang Qishan and Zhang Gaoli.
- **Anti-corruption campaign.** Led by Wang Qishan as head of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI), it targeted both "tigers and flies": senior and junior officials. CCDI deputy secretary Huang Shuxian told a Beijing press conference in January 2014 (reported by China Daily) that disciplinary authorities had punished 182,038 officials in 2013, 13.3 percent more than in 2012. That is an official, unaudited party figure.
- **Senior targets.** Former security chief Zhou Yongkang was placed under investigation in July 2014 and expelled from the party in December 2014. The earlier Bo Xilai affair (2012) formed the background to the leadership transition.
- **Reform agenda.** The Third Plenum (November 2013) promised a "decisive role" for the market.
- **Social controls.** Tighter controls on expression included the "Seven Don'ts" directive, reported in 2013. Some analysts, such as those writing for the Jamestown Foundation, questioned whether the anti-corruption campaign was also a political purge.

**External relations.** China:
- Declared the East China Sea air defence identification zone.
- Contested the Scarborough Shoal and moved an oil rig near the Paracels in May–June 2014, which triggered anti-Chinese riots in Vietnam.
- Proposed the Silk Road initiatives and the AIIB.
- Hosted the APEC summit in Beijing (November 2014), where it concluded the climate announcement with the US.

**Economy.** China was the largest manufacturer, a very large exporter, and the main customer for many commodity exporters. The CRS summary of IMF figures puts average growth at about 7.6 percent for 2012–14. Economists inside and outside China questioned the reliability of provincial and national statistics.

**Knowledge.** Chinese achievements included Chang'e-3 and Yutu, the Shenzhou-10 crewed flight and Tiangong-1 docking (2013), the Tianhe-2 supercomputer ranking first on the TOP500 list from 2013, and a fast-growing output of scientific papers. Mo Yan won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2012.

**Hong Kong.** The Occupy Central / Umbrella protests of September–December 2014 were a response to Beijing's August 2014 framework for electing the Chief Executive. Leaders included Joshua Wong, Benny Tai and Alex Chow. The protests ended without concessions.

**Key organisations.** The CCDI, the People's Bank of China (governor Zhou Xiaochuan), state-owned enterprises such as Sinopec, PetroChina and State Grid, and private platforms such as Alibaba (Jack Ma), Tencent (Pony Ma) and Huawei.

### United States

**Internal politics.**
- Obama won re-election in November 2012.
- Automatic budget cuts ("sequestration") took effect in 2013, and the government shut down in October 2013.
- The launch of the Affordable Care Act was contested.
- Republicans won the Senate in the November 2014 midterms.
- Protests after the police killing of Michael Brown in Ferguson in August 2014 put race and policing at the centre of debate.

**The Snowden disclosures (from June 2013).** These exposed the NSA's mass-surveillance programmes. They strained relations with Germany and Brazil (Dilma Rousseff postponed a state visit) and fed global debates about data sovereignty.

**External relations.**
- The Asia "rebalance".
- In August–September 2013 the US threatened strikes over the Ghouta chemical attack, then agreed a US–Russia framework to remove Syria's chemical weapons.
- The Iran talks.
- Sanctions on Russia.
- The campaign against ISIL from August 2014.
- The announcement of normalisation with Cuba in December 2014.

**Economy.** Recovery was slow. The Federal Reserve (chaired by Ben Bernanke, then Janet Yellen from 2014) ran a third round of quantitative easing (QE3) and ended asset purchases in October 2014. The shale boom made the US a rapidly growing oil producer.

### European Union and eurozone

- **Internal.** ECB President Mario Draghi's July 2012 pledge and the Outright Monetary Transactions programme calmed bond markets. Other developments:
  - Cyprus received a bail-in rescue in March 2013.
  - Banking union began, with the ECB taking over bank supervision in November 2014.
  - Croatia joined the EU in July 2013.
  - Anti-establishment parties made gains in the May 2014 European Parliament elections.
  - Jean-Claude Juncker's Commission took office in November 2014.
- **Key figures.** Angela Merkel was the pivotal national leader. François Hollande ordered interventions in Mali (January 2013) and the Central African Republic (December 2013).
- **External.** The EU led the Association Agreement with Ukraine, whose suspension by Yanukovych in November 2013 triggered Euromaidan. It negotiated the Transatlantic Trade and Investment Partnership (TTIP) with the US and applied sanctions on Russia in stages.
- **Knowledge.** CERN's Higgs result (Peter Higgs and François Englert shared the 2013 Nobel Prize) and Rosetta/Philae.

### Russia

**Internal.** Putin returned amid the 2011–12 protests led by figures such as Alexei Navalny. New laws followed on "foreign agents" (2012) and on "gay propaganda" (2013). Russia hosted the Sochi Winter Olympics in February 2014.

**External.**
- **Eurasian Economic Union.** The treaty was signed in May 2014 with Belarus and Kazakhstan.
- **Crimea.** Annexed after a March 2014 referendum that Ukraine and most UN members rejected. A UN General Assembly resolution affirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity.
- **Donbas.** Support for the separatists was denied officially, though UN monitors reported an inflow of weapons and fighters "including from the Russian Federation".
- **MH17.** Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 was shot down over the Donbas on 17 July 2014, killing 298 people. Responsibility was contested between Kyiv, Moscow and the separatists; the Dutch-led investigation was still under way at end-2014.
- **Gas deal.** A 30-year gas contract was signed with China in May 2014.

**Russian framing.** Official and state media described Ukraine's new authorities as the product of an illegitimate coup, and the Crimea vote as self-determination.

**Economy.** Russia depended on hydrocarbons. Oil at $57 and sanctions pushed the Central Bank (chair Elvira Nabiullina) to raise its key rate to 17 percent in December 2014.

### India

**Internal.** The late period of Manmohan Singh's second UPA government (UPA-II) was dominated by corruption scandals over the 2G telecom and coal allocations. Other developments:
- Anna Hazare's anti-corruption movement gave rise to Arvind Kejriwal's Aam Aadmi Party, which briefly governed Delhi from December 2013.
- Protests after the December 2012 gang-rape led to criminal-law amendments in 2013.
- Telangana was created as a new state in 2014.

**The 2014 election.** The BJP took 282 seats. Congress, led in the campaign by Rahul Gandhi, fell to 44, its worst result.
- **Interpretation.** Analysts at Lokniti-CSDS and elsewhere debated whether this was a vote for Modi personally or a national realignment. The BJP's 31 percent was the lowest vote share ever to produce a single-party majority.
- **Regional parties.** Jayalalithaa's AIADMK (37 seats) and Mamata Banerjee's Trinamool Congress (34) remained major forces.

**Economy.** Growth slowed and inflation was high in 2012–13. The rupee came under severe pressure in the 2013 taper tantrum, and Raghuvir (Raghuram) Rajan took over the Reserve Bank in September 2013.

**Knowledge.** The Indian Space Research Organisation's (ISRO) Mars Orbiter Mission entered orbit on its first attempt in September 2014. Kailash Satyarthi shared the 2014 Nobel Peace Prize with Malala Yousafzai.

### Japan

Abe's programme of "Abenomics" had three "arrows": aggressive easing by the Bank of Japan under Haruhiko Kuroda (from 2013), fiscal stimulus and structural reform. Other developments:
- A consumption tax rise in April 2014 was followed by recession and a snap election in December 2014, which kept Abe in power.
- The government reinterpreted the constitution in July 2014 to allow collective self-defence.
- It nationalised three Senkaku islands in September 2012, prompting large protests in China.
- Tokyo won the 2020 Olympics (2013).
- The Fukushima aftermath kept most nuclear reactors offline.

### Middle East and North Africa

**Egypt.**
- **Political sequence.** Morsi (Muslim Brotherhood) was elected in June 2012. His November 2012 constitutional declaration provoked mass opposition, and the Tamarod petition campaign gathered support. After huge protests on 30 June 2013 the military, under Defence Minister Sisi, removed him on 3 July.
- **Rabaa and al-Nahda dispersals, 14 August 2013.** Counts are contested. Human Rights Watch documented at least 817 killed at Rabaa, "more likely at least 1,000". The Health Ministry reported 638 killed including 43 police. The National Council for Human Rights reported 632. The Brotherhood-aligned coalition claimed about 2,600.
- **Afterwards.** The Brotherhood was designated a terrorist organisation (December 2013), Sisi was elected in May 2014, and Gulf financial support flowed in.

**Syria.** The war was fought between the Assad government (backed by Iran, Hezbollah and Russian diplomacy) and a fragmented opposition (the Free Syrian Army, Islamist coalitions and Jabhat al-Nusra), with ISIL and the Kurdish YPG as further actors.
- **Chemical weapons.** The Ghouta attack (21 August 2013) led to the disarmament deal.
- **Talks.** The Geneva II talks (January–February 2014) failed.
- **Kobani.** The siege began in September 2014.
- **Casualty counts.** The UN human rights office stopped updating its death count in January 2014, citing lack of access. It resumed in August 2014 with the Human Rights Data Analysis Group's figure of 191,369 documented killings to 30 April 2014. The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights published higher running totals, and the UN did not endorse any other count.

**Iraq.** Sunni protests in 2013 were followed by ISIL's capture of Fallujah (January 2014) and Mosul (10 June 2014). Other events:
- The Sinjar atrocities against Yazidis (August 2014).
- Grand Ayatollah Ali al-Sistani's call to arms, which mobilised volunteer militias.
- Haider al-Abadi replaced Nouri al-Maliki (August–September 2014).
- The UN mission in Iraq (UNAMI) recorded at least 12,282 civilians killed in 2014, which it said "must be considered the absolute minimum". The deadliest month was June, with 1,531 killed.

**Iran.** Rouhani and Foreign Minister Mohammad Javad Zarif negotiated the JPOA with the P5+1 powers in November 2013. Deadlines for a final deal were extended in July and November 2014; its outcome was unknown. The Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, set its limits.

**The Gulf.** Saudi Arabia (King Abdullah; oil minister Ali al-Naimi) led OPEC's November 2014 decision. Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Bahrain withdrew their ambassadors from Qatar in March 2014 over its support for the Brotherhood. The Gulf states used their sovereign wealth to support allies in Egypt and elsewhere.

**Turkey.** The Gezi protests (May–June 2013) were followed by corruption investigations in December 2013, which Erdoğan blamed on the Gülen movement. Erdoğan won the presidency in the first direct election in August 2014, and Ahmet Davutoğlu became prime minister. Peace talks with the PKK were ongoing.

**Israel and Palestine.**
- November 2012 brought a Gaza conflict and Palestine's upgrade to UN non-member observer state.
- US-brokered talks collapsed in April 2014.
- The 2014 Gaza war (8 July–26 August) followed. UN OCHA and the UN Commission of Inquiry counted 2,251 Palestinians killed, including 1,462 civilians and 551 children. Israeli sources gave figures ranging from 67 soldiers and 6 civilians killed to 66 soldiers and 5 civilians.
- The civilian share of Palestinian deaths was contested. Israel's military counted 2,127 dead and assessed that a much larger share were combatants.

**Libya, Yemen and Tunisia.**
- **Libya.** Fragmented into rival coalitions in 2014: "Operation Dignity" under Khalifa Haftar, and "Libya Dawn". Rival parliaments sat in Tobruk and Tripoli.
- **Yemen.** A National Dialogue ran from 2013 to 2014, then the Houthis took Sanaa in September 2014.
- **Tunisia.** Adopted a consensus constitution in January 2014 and held elections, a transition that the civil-society mediation "Quartet" helped broker.

### Sub-Saharan Africa

**Nigeria.** Goodluck Jonathan's government faced an escalating Boko Haram insurgency in the northeast under Abubakar Shekau. A state of emergency covered three states from May 2013.
- **Chibok.** Official counts changed over time. The Borno government first reported 129 abducted. The military falsely claimed most had been freed, then retracted the claim. Parents said more than 300. By May–June 2014 police and a presidential committee settled on 276 abducted, 57 escaped and 219 held.
- **Fatalities in 2014 varied by what was counted.** Amnesty International counted at least 4,000 civilians killed in at least 230 attacks. Human Rights Watch counted more than 2,563 killed. The Global Terrorism Index (2015) attributed 6,644 deaths to Boko Haram, counting terrorism deaths only.
- **Economy.** Nigeria's economy rested on oil. Finance minister Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala and statistician-general Yemi Kale led the rebasing. Central Bank governor Lamido Sanusi was suspended in February 2014 after alleging missing oil revenues.

**South Africa.** Police killed 34 striking miners at Marikana in August 2012. Nelson Mandela died on 5 December 2013. The ANC under Jacob Zuma won the May 2014 election, while Julius Malema's new Economic Freedom Fighters entered parliament.

**Mali and the Sahel.**
- **2012 crisis.** Tuareg separatists (the MNLA) and jihadist groups (AQIM, Ansar Dine and MUJAO) seized the north in 2012, and a coup struck Bamako in March 2012.
- **French intervention.** France launched Operation Serval on 11 January 2013 after armed groups took Konna.
- **UN mission.** UN Security Council Resolution 2100 (25 April 2013) created the peacekeeping mission MINUSMA, which took over on 1 July 2013 with up to 11,200 troops.
- **Agreement and election.** The Ouagadougou Preliminary Agreement (18 June 2013) allowed Ibrahim Boubacar Keïta to be elected president.
- **Regional operation.** Serval was succeeded by Operation Barkhane across the Sahel in August 2014.

**Central African Republic.** The Séléka coalition took Bangui in March 2013. Its violence, and the counter-violence of anti-balaka militias, took on a religious dimension. Interventions came from France (Sangaris), the African Union mission (MISCA) and, from September 2014, the UN mission MINUSCA.
- **Deaths.** The UN Commission of Inquiry (December 2014) said it could not establish the toll "with any degree of accuracy". It noted existing estimates of 3,000–6,000 killed, which it said "fail to capture the full magnitude".
- **Displacement.** UNHCR (5 December 2014) counted more than 850,000 displaced: about 430,000 internally and more than 423,000 as refugees. This was down from about 935,000 IDPs in January 2014.

**South Sudan.** Fighting between President Salva Kiir and former vice-president Riek Machar began in Juba in December 2013 and spread along lines between Dinka and Nuer communities.
- **Death toll.** Estimates were guesswork. The International Crisis Group suggested nearly 10,000 dead by January 2014, and "at least 50,000" circulated from 2014. The government disputed high figures.
- **Peace talks.** The regional bloc IGAD mediated talks in Addis Ababa.

**East Africa and the Horn.**
- **Kenya.** Uhuru Kenyatta was elected in 2013 while under indictment at the International Criminal Court. His case was withdrawn in December 2014.
- **Westgate.** Al-Shabaab attacked the Westgate mall in Nairobi in September 2013.
- **Somalia.** A federal government was formed in 2012.
- **Ethiopia.** After Meles Zenawi died in August 2012, Hailemariam Desalegn took over. The government pursued state-led growth and began the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, which Egypt disputed.

**Other.** In Burkina Faso, a popular uprising ended Blaise Compaoré's 27-year rule in October 2014.

### Latin America

**Brazil.**
- The June 2013 protests began over bus fares and widened to public services and the cost of the World Cup.
- The Lava Jato investigation into Petrobras contracts began in March 2014.
- Brazil hosted the World Cup in June–July 2014.
- Dilma Rousseff (PT) won re-election in October 2014 in a narrow second round against Aécio Neves (PSDB).
- The economy was slowing as commodity prices fell.

**Venezuela.** Chávez died in March 2013 and Nicolás Maduro won a close election the following month. The 2014 street protests ("La Salida", with figures such as Leopoldo López, who was jailed) coincided with high inflation, shortages and falling oil revenue.

**Mexico.** Enrique Peña Nieto's "Pact for Mexico" produced reforms including the 2013 energy reform. The disappearance of 43 students from the Ayotzinapa teacher-training college in September 2014 triggered mass protests over collusion between officials and organised crime.

**Colombia, Argentina and Cuba.**
- **Colombia.** The Santos government negotiated with the FARC in Havana from 2012 and Santos was re-elected in 2014.
- **Argentina.** Cristina Fernández de Kirchner's government fought the holdout creditors (the NML case) and defaulted in July 2014.
- **Cuba.** Began market reforms under Raúl Castro and announced the US thaw in December 2014.

### South and Southeast Asia, Korea and Central Asia

- **Pakistan.** Nawaz Sharif won in 2013, the first transfer of power between elected governments. The army's Zarb-e-Azb offensive in North Waziristan began in June 2014. Imran Khan and Tahir-ul-Qadri led sit-ins in Islamabad in 2014. The Tehrik-i-Taliban's attack on the Army Public School in Peshawar on 16 December 2014 killed at least 141 people, including 132 children and nine staff, according to Pakistani military spokesman Maj. Gen. Asim Bajwa (Express Tribune).
- **Bangladesh.** The Shahbag protests (2013) demanded war-crimes executions. Rana Plaza collapsed on 24 April 2013, killing at least 1,134 people and injuring more than 2,500, according to the ILO. The January 2014 election was boycotted by the opposition BNP.
- **Afghanistan.** The 2014 runoff between Ashraf Ghani and Abdullah Abdullah was disputed. A US-brokered unity government was formed in September, and ISAF ended in December 2014.
- **Myanmar.** Political opening under Thein Sein continued: Aung San Suu Kyi entered parliament in 2012. Anti-Rohingya and anti-Muslim violence in Rakhine State in June and October 2012 displaced an estimated 140,000 people, according to UN OCHA.
- **Thailand.** Protests against Yingluck Shinawatra's government (led by Suthep Thaugsuban) preceded the May 2014 coup under General Prayut Chan-o-cha.
- **Indonesia.** Jokowi, a furniture businessman and former mayor of Solo, beat Prabowo, a former special-forces commander. Prabowo challenged the result in the Constitutional Court and lost.
- **Vietnam and the Philippines.** Both confronted China at sea. Benigno Aquino III's Philippines filed the arbitration case.
- **Malaysia.** The disappearance of flight MH370 in March 2014 remained unexplained.
- **South Korea.** The Sewol ferry sank on 16 April 2014, killing 304 people, including 250 students from Danwon High School (Associated Press). It triggered a crisis of public trust.
- **North Korea.** Kim Jong-un consolidated power, including through the execution of his uncle, Jang Song-thaek.

## IV. THEMATIC SECTIONS

### Knowledge, science and technology

**Physics and space.**
- The Higgs discovery (ATLAS and CMS at CERN, July 2012).
- NASA's Curiosity rover landed on Mars in August 2012.
- Chang'e-3 and Yutu, India's Mars mission and Rosetta/Philae, as described above.
- In March 2014 the BICEP2 team claimed to have detected primordial gravitational waves. Analyses in 2014, including Planck results on dust, challenged the claim, and it was unresolved at year-end.

**Life sciences.** Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier's 2012 paper, followed by Feng Zhang's and George Church's 2013 work in human cells, made CRISPR-Cas9 editing widely usable. Ethical debate had begun. Japan's 2014 STAP stem-cell papers were retracted after misconduct findings at the RIKEN research institute. The event was seen in Japan as a crisis of research integrity.

**Computing and communications.**
- Deep learning's ImageNet result (University of Toronto, 2012) and big-tech investment in it were noticed mainly by specialists.
- Smartphone adoption was very fast in China, India, Africa and Latin America.
- WeChat grew rapidly, and Facebook bought WhatsApp in February 2014.
- After Snowden, Brazil's Marco Civil da Internet (2014) and European data-protection debates were the main policy responses.

**Energy.** Horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing transformed US output. Solar photovoltaic costs fell steeply, driven largely by Chinese manufacturing.

### Health and population

- **Ebola.** The epidemic overwhelmed health systems in Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea. WHO's slow early response was widely criticised, and the UN set up UNMEER, its first emergency health mission, in September 2014. By WHO's December 2014 reports, the case fatality rate among cases with recorded outcomes was about 70 percent in the three most-affected countries, and 649 health workers had been infected (ECDC summary).
- **Other outbreaks.** MERS, H7N9 and the polio emergency (Pakistan, Syria and Cameroon were cited as exporting cases).
- **Longer-term trends.** Deaths from HIV, malaria and child mortality continued to decline under the Millennium Development Goals framework, though measurement varies by source.
- **Demographic pressures.** Ageing was the dominant trend in Japan, Europe and, increasingly, China, which relaxed its one-child policy in November 2013. High fertility persisted in Sub-Saharan Africa.

### Security and conflict

**Casualty counts.** All figures below are institutional counts with known limits:
- Syria: UN human rights office, 191,369 documented killings to April 2014.
- Iraq: UNAMI, at least 12,282 civilians killed in 2014, against 7,818 in 2013.
- Eastern Ukraine: UN human rights office and WHO, at least 4,707 killed from mid-April to 12 December 2014.
- Gaza 2014: OCHA, 2,251 Palestinians killed.
- Central African Republic: 3,000–6,000 killed, from existing estimates the UN Commission of Inquiry judged too low.
- South Sudan: tens of thousands killed, with no systematic count.
- Nigeria: counts in the thousands, depending on method.

**Military technique.** Drone strikes (Pakistan, Yemen, Somalia), "hybrid" methods (unmarked forces in Crimea) and the heavy use of social media for propaganda (ISIL) were widely discussed. Chemical weapons were used in Syria in 2013.

**Military spending.** SIPRI recorded falling spending in North America and Western Europe, and rising spending in Asia, the Middle East, Eastern Europe and Africa.

### Culture and society

**Where culture was made.** Cultural production had many centres and was increasingly digital. Examples include K-pop's global reach (PSY's "Gangnam Style", 2012), Nollywood (valued at about 1.2 percent of Nigeria's rebased GDP), Bollywood and China's growing box office.

**Recurring themes.**
- Protests over corruption, inequality and dignity.
- Debates over women's rights and gender violence (India; the abduction of the Chibok girls and the #BringBackOurGirls campaign).
- LGBT rights: same-sex marriage spread in the Americas and Europe, while Uganda, Nigeria and Russia passed restrictive laws in 2013–14.
- Religious authority and sectarian mobilisation in the Muslim world.

**Recognition.** Malala Yousafzai's Nobel award and Pope Francis's emphasis on poverty and the "peripheries" were seen as symbolic of this period's moral debates.

## V. CONCLUSION

The world of 2012–2014 is best described as one in transition without a settled direction. The live questions at the end of 2014 were:
- whether China's reform and anti-corruption drive would deliver a soft landing;
- whether the eurozone had escaped its crisis;
- whether Minsk would hold in Ukraine, and how far sanctions would change Russian policy;
- whether the Iran talks would produce a final deal;
- whether ISIL could be contained, and by whom;
- whether West Africa could stop Ebola;
- how long low oil prices would last, and what that meant for producers from Venezuela to Nigeria to Russia;
- whether newly elected leaders in India and Indonesia would carry out their reform promises.

Each of these had several possible outcomes. That is the proper way to treat this period for any further analysis or simulation.
